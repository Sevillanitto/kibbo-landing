Insurance & Claims

Regulators, complaints and ombudsman services, claims and dispute resolution, policy and coverage guidance, and motor, home, travel, and health insurance — for the US, UK, EU, and Australia.

← All free resources

Insurance regulation splits along familiar lines: the US regulates insurance state-by-state with no single federal insurance regulator; the EU's EIOPA supervises at EU level but does not handle individual complaints directly — those go through national regulators or the FIN-NET cross-border network; and Australia splits prudential regulation (APRA) from complaint handling (AFCA) entirely, with private health insurance carved out to a separate ombudsman again. This directory keeps "who regulates" and "who handles a complaint" as deliberately separate sections, since they're rarely the same body.

Need a document instead? Our templates and generators cover claim evidence, policy comparison, damage/loss inventories, denial disputes, and third-party liability claims, and our free checklists walk through the same ground step by step.

Section 1 · Insurance Regulators & Consumer Protection

Who actually regulates insurers in each region — the starting point before filing a complaint (see Section 2 for that).

Find your state insurance regulator in the US

NAIC — National Association of Insurance Commissioners content.naic.org →

What: Insurance is regulated state-by-state in the US — there is no single federal insurance regulator. NAIC coordinates the state regulators and runs a directory of every state insurance department and a consumer complaint tool.

Find your specific state's Department of Insurance

NAIC — State Insurance Departments Directory content.naic.org — State Insurance Departments →

What: Linked here via NAIC's directory rather than 50 separate state links — find your specific state's Department of Insurance from this single starting point.

Understand insurance regulation and your rights in the UK

FCA — Financial Conduct Authority (Consumers) fca.org.uk/consumers →

What: The UK's single financial services regulator, covering insurance alongside banking and other financial products. This consumer hub covers rights, how to complain, and how to check a firm is actually authorised.

Understand EU-level insurance supervision

EIOPA — European Insurance and Occupational Pensions Authority eiopa.europa.eu — Consumer protection →

What: EIOPA supervises insurers and promotes consumer protection standards at EU level — but it does not handle individual consumer complaints directly. For an actual complaint, use your national regulator or FIN-NET (Section 2).

Understand your consumer insurance rights in Australia

ASIC — Australian Securities and Investments Commission asic.gov.au — Insurance for consumers →

What: ASIC's consumer-facing insurance section, covering how insurance regulation works and where to go for help.

Understand insurer prudential regulation in Australia

APRA — Australian Prudential Regulation Authority apra.gov.au →

What: APRA prudentially regulates insurers — making sure they're financially sound enough to pay claims — but does not handle individual complaints. Those go to AFCA (Section 2), except private health insurance, which goes to the Private Health Insurance Ombudsman (Section 8).

Section 2 · Insurance Complaints & Ombudsman Services

Escalate a complaint once your insurer's own internal process hasn't resolved it.

Check your insurer's own complaints process first

What: There's no single link for this — check your own insurer's policy documents or website for its internal complaints process. In most jurisdictions, you must give the insurer a chance to resolve the complaint internally before an external body like an ombudsman will accept it (the UK's FOS, for example, generally requires this).

File a complaint against your insurer in the US

NAIC — How to File a Complaint content.naic.org — How to file a complaint →

What: Routes you to your specific state's Department of Insurance complaint tool — complaint handling is state-based in the US, with no single national body.

Escalate an insurance complaint in the UK

Financial Ombudsman Service (FOS) financial-ombudsman.org.uk →

What: Free, independent complaint resolution for insurance and other financial services. The insurer must be given a chance to resolve the complaint first — generally up to 8 weeks — before FOS can step in.

Resolve a cross-border insurance complaint in the EU/EEA

FIN-NET — Financial Dispute Resolution Network finance.ec.europa.eu — FIN-NET →

What: A network of national out-of-court dispute-resolution bodies for cross-border financial services complaints across the EU/EEA (EU member states plus Iceland, Liechtenstein, and Norway).

Escalate an insurance complaint in Australia

AFCA — Australian Financial Complaints Authority afca.org.au →

What: Free, binding dispute resolution for insurance and other financial complaints. AFCA does not cover private health insurance — those complaints go to the Private Health Insurance Ombudsman instead (Section 8).

Section 4 · Insurance Policy & Coverage Resources

Understand how policies, coverage, and exclusions actually work before you buy, renew, or rely on one.

Learn how insurance policies actually work in the US

III — Insurance Information Institute iii.org →

What: A nonprofit, industry-funded but editorially independent consumer education resource on how policies, coverage, and exclusions work across every major insurance type.

Get consumer guidance on buying insurance in the UK

ABI — Association of British Insurers, How to Buy Insurance abi.org.uk — How to buy insurance →

What: The UK insurance industry's trade body, offering consumer-facing guidance on choosing and understanding a policy — industry-funded, so read alongside independent sources like Citizens Advice or the FCA.

Understand policyholder protection at EU level

EIOPA — Consumer Protection eiopa.europa.eu — Consumer protection →

What: The same reference as Section 1, relevant here for how EIOPA's standards shape what a policy in the EU must disclose about coverage and exclusions.

Get consumer insurance guidance in Australia

Moneysmart (ASIC) moneysmart.gov.au — Insurance →

What: ASIC's consumer money website, covering how to choose insurance and understand what different policy types actually cover.

Section 5 · Motor Insurance

Insurance and uninsured/untraced-driver resources specific to vehicles. For buying, registering, or disputing a vehicle itself, see the Cars & Vehicles directory.

Claim against an uninsured or untraced driver in the UK

Motor Insurers' Bureau (MIB) mib.org.uk →

What: A statutory compensation mechanism, funded by a levy on every UK motor insurer, for accidents caused by uninsured or untraced (hit-and-run) drivers.

Check if a specific UK vehicle is insured

askMID askmid.com →

What: A free service, run by MIB, to check whether a specific vehicle is currently insured by its registration number.

Understand uninsured motorist coverage in the US

NAIC — Uninsured Motorists content.naic.org — Uninsured motorists →

What: Explains uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage, since there's no single national body like the UK's MIB in the US — this coverage is bought as part of your own policy and regulated state-by-state.

Understand compulsory third party (CTP) insurance in Australia (example: NSW)

SIRA — Compulsory Third Party (Green Slip) Insurance sira.nsw.gov.au — How to buy a Green Slip →

What: CTP insurance is compulsory in every Australian state and territory, but it's state-administered — NSW's "Green Slip" scheme (regulated by SIRA) is shown as a representative example; check your own state or territory's equivalent scheme and regulator.

See the Cars & Vehicles directory for the vehicle side of a claim

What: Vehicle registration, VIN/history checks, recalls, and dealer/repair disputes live in the Cars & Vehicles directory's Insurance & Accident Claims section, along with its own checklists and templates.

Section 6 · Home & Property Insurance

Home, contents, and natural-disaster insurance guidance. For the repair or renovation process itself, see the Home Renovations & Services directory.

Learn how home and renters insurance works in the US

III — Homeowners + Renters Insurance iii.org — Homeowners + Renters →

What: Coverage types, what's typically included, and what standard policies exclude — see the flood note below for the biggest gap.

Understand flood insurance in the US

NFIP — National Flood Insurance Program floodsmart.gov →

What: The official, FEMA-administered flood insurance program. Standard home insurance policies typically exclude flood damage entirely — this is bought separately.

Get flood and natural disaster claim guidance in the UK

ABI — Recovering From a Flood abi.org.uk — Recovering from a flood →

What: Practical step-by-step guidance for starting an insurance claim and rebuilding after a flood, plus background on the Flood Re scheme that keeps flood cover available in high-risk areas.

Get natural disaster insurance guidance in Australia

Insurance Council of Australia — Help in Disasters insurancecouncil.com.au — Help in disasters →

What: The insurance industry body's practical guidance for policyholders during and after a declared catastrophe, including how the Insurance Hub and catastrophe declarations work.

See Home Renovations & Services for the repair side

Section 8 · Health & Other Insurance

Health, life, and pet insurance — kept separate from Sections 1–2 since health insurance often has its own dedicated regulator or complaint body.

Find official health insurance information in the US

HealthCare.gov healthcare.gov →

What: The official US government health insurance marketplace and consumer information site.

Understand private medical insurance regulation in the UK

FCA — Financial Conduct Authority (Consumers) fca.org.uk/consumers →

What: The same general consumer hub as Section 1 — the FCA regulates private medical insurance (PMI) as a general insurance product, not through a dedicated PMI-specific page. Because of the NHS, private health insurance in the UK is optional supplementary cover, not the primary route to healthcare as it is in the US.

Complain about private health insurance in Australia

Private Health Insurance Ombudsman (run by the Commonwealth Ombudsman) privatehealth.gov.au →

What: This is a different body from AFCA (Section 2) — private health insurance complaints in Australia go here, not to AFCA. Also the official source of independent private health insurance information for consumers.

Life insurance

What: Life insurance is regulated by the same general bodies covered in Sections 1 and 4 — NAIC in the US, the ABI/FCA in the UK — not duplicated again here.

Understand pet insurance in the US

NAIC — Pet Insurance content.naic.org — Pet insurance →

What: NAIC's consumer topic page on pet insurance, including what a policy typically covers and how pre-existing conditions are usually treated.

See the Healthcare & Medical directory

What: For health insurance claims and disputes specifically, see the Healthcare & Medical directory's Health Insurance & Claims section.
