Training & Education

Who regulates a school, how to check a qualification is real, and where a student actually goes to complain — organized by function, not blended together.

← All free resources

Education is regulated differently depending on the country, the level of study, and whether the provider is public, private, or online-only. There is no single global authority for any of this. These are the official sources for each function, organized by what they actually do.

Section 1 · Education Regulators & Authorities

The national body that sets standards or oversees education providers in each region — the starting point before looking for anything more specific.

US — Federal Trade Commission

U.S. Federal Trade Commission ftc.gov →

What: The FTC enforces truth-in-advertising rules against for-profit schools and bootcamps — false job placement rates, deceptive enrollment tactics, and unfair loan terms fall under its jurisdiction.

US — Consumer Financial Protection Bureau

U.S. Consumer Financial Protection Bureau consumerfinance.gov →

What: The CFPB regulates student loans and income share agreements as credit products. For its complaint-filing tool specifically, see Section 5.

US — Department of Education

U.S. Department of Education ed.gov →

What: Sets federal education policy and administers federal student aid. Its accreditation database is a separate tool — see Section 2.

UK — Ofsted

Office for Standards in Education, Children's Services and Skills gov.uk/ofsted →

What: Inspects and regulates schools, further education, and childcare providers in England.

UK — Office for Students

Office for Students officeforstudents.org.uk →

What: The independent regulator of higher education in England — registers providers and sets conditions around quality, access, and value for money.

UK — Department for Education

UK Department for Education gov.uk — Department for Education →

What: The Education and Skills Funding Agency (ESFA) closed permanently on 31 March 2025 — its functions (including apprenticeship and further-education funding oversight) were absorbed directly into the Department for Education. This is the current department to go to; ESFA is defunct and no longer a live regulator.

Australia — ASQA

Australian Skills Quality Authority asqa.gov.au →

What: The national regulator for Australia's vocational education and training sector, including RTOs (Registered Training Organisations) and bootcamps.

EU — European Commission (Education & Training)

European Education Area — European Commission education.ec.europa.eu →

What: The European Commission's hub for EU-wide education policy. Actual accreditation and licensing is run by each member state — this is the entry point, not a single national regulator.

Section 2 · Accreditation & Qualification Databases

Check whether a school or qualification is actually accredited before you pay for it — a different function from the regulators in Section 1.

US — CHEA

Council for Higher Education Accreditation chea.org →

What: The nonprofit that recognizes legitimate US accrediting bodies and maintains a public database of accredited institutions and programs — a first stop for spotting a diploma mill.

US — DAPIP (Dept. of Education accreditation database)

Database of Accredited Postsecondary Institutions and Programs ope.ed.gov/dapip →

What: The US Department of Education's own searchable database of institutions and programs accredited by recognized accrediting agencies. This URL has moved before — confirmed current as of this publish.

Australia — CRICOS (accreditation lookup)

Commonwealth Register of Institutions and Courses for Overseas Students cricos.education.gov.au →

What: The official registry of Australian institutions and courses approved to enrol international students. Listed again in Section 4 for its visa-verification use — the search intent differs (checking accreditation here vs. confirming visa-sponsor eligibility there), not a duplicate.

Spain — RUCT

Registro de Universidades, Centros y Títulos universidades.sede.gob.es — RUCT →

What: Spain's official public registry of universities, centres, and official degree titles (Grado, Máster, Doctorado). The older educacion.gob.es/ruct/home URL is outdated — this sede.gob.es address is the current live registry.

EU — European Qualifications Framework (EQF)

European Qualifications Framework europass.europa.eu — the EQF →

What: A reference framework for comparing qualification levels (1–8) across European countries — not a searchable accreditation database. Use it to understand what a foreign qualification level actually means, not to look up a specific institution.

Section 4 · Student Visa & International Study Verification

Confirm a school is actually approved to sponsor an international student before you enroll or apply for a visa — distinct from general accreditation in Section 2.

Australia — CRICOS (visa-sponsor lookup)

Commonwealth Register of Institutions and Courses for Overseas Students cricos.education.gov.au →

What: The same register as Section 2, listed again here because the use case is different: confirming a provider can legally enrol a student visa holder, not checking general accreditation.

Australia — Tuition Protection Service

Tuition Protection Service (TPS) education.gov.au — Tuition Protection Service →

What: Protects international (and some domestic) students if their education provider can't deliver a course — arranges a replacement course or refund. Listed again in Section 5 for its complaint/relocation role.

UK — Register of Licensed Sponsors (Student route)

UK Visas and Immigration — Register of Licensed Sponsors: students gov.uk — register of licensed sponsors: students →

What: The official, daily-updated list of UK institutions licensed to sponsor Student and Child Student route visas. If a school isn't on this list, it cannot legally sponsor a student visa.

US — SEVP School Search

Study in the States — SEVP School Search studyinthestates.dhs.gov — school search →

What: DHS's official tool for confirming a school is SEVP-certified and can issue the I-20 required for an F-1 or M-1 student visa.

Section 5 · Student Complaint & Ombudsman Bodies

Where a student actually goes to complain once the school or platform hasn't resolved it — distinct from the regulators and accreditation checkers above.

UK — Office of the Independent Adjudicator (OIA)

Office of the Independent Adjudicator for Higher Education oiahe.org.uk →

What: The designated, free student complaints scheme for higher education providers in England and Wales — the official next step once a university's own complaints process is exhausted.

Australia — Tuition Protection Service

Tuition Protection Service (TPS) education.gov.au — Tuition Protection Service →

What: Same body as Section 4, listed again here because it also handles the complaint and relocation process when a provider closes or fails to deliver — not a duplicate, a second function of the same service.

UK — Financial Ombudsman Service

Financial Ombudsman Service financial-ombudsman.org.uk →

What: Handles disputes over linked or connected credit used to pay for education — for example a course financed through a third-party loan or buy-now-pay-later agreement, where the credit provider shares liability with the school.

US — CFPB complaint portal (student loans)

Consumer Financial Protection Bureau — Submit a complaint consumerfinance.gov — submit a complaint →

What: The CFPB's complaint tool, specifically useful for student loan and income share agreement disputes — a different function from the general regulator entry in Section 1.

Section 6 · Europass & EU Qualification Recognition

Turn a qualification or work history into a format employers and institutions across the EU actually recognize — a different use case from the accreditation databases in Section 2.

EU — Europass

Europass europass.europa.eu →

What: The EU's official platform for building a CV, documenting skills, and generating recognized digital credentials that are understood consistently across EU member states — a recognition and presentation tool, not an accreditation checker.
