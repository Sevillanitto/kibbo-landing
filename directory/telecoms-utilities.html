Telecoms & Utilities

Regulators, ombudsman schemes, complaint portals, and provider cancellation pages for telecom, energy, and water — for the US, UK, EU, and Australia.

← All free resources

A telecom or utility problem splits into genuinely different questions — who sets the rules for this market, who resolves an individual dispute, where you actually file a complaint, and how you cancel a service that won't let you leave. This directory keeps those apart deliberately: a regulator overseeing a market is not the same as an ombudsman resolving your specific case, and neither is the same as the provider's own cancellation page.

Coverage is primarily US, UK, and Australia, where dedicated ombudsman and complaint-portal infrastructure exists. The EU is included where a genuine EU-level body exists (BEREC for regulation, ECC-Net for cross-border complaints) — there is no single EU-wide consumer energy/water regulator or ombudsman, since each member state runs its own.

Section 1 · Telecom Regulators Directory

The bodies that set the rules for telecom markets — not necessarily who resolves your individual complaint (see Section 3).

The US federal telecom regulator

Federal Communications Commission (FCC) fcc.gov/consumers →

What: The US federal regulator for phone, internet, cable, and radio services — billing practices, coverage obligations, service quality rules, and number porting.

When to use: To understand what a US telecom provider is actually required to do before you file a complaint (Section 4).

The UK telecom regulator

Ofcom (UK) ofcom.org.uk →

What: UK regulator for broadband, mobile, landline, and postal services. Sets the rules providers must follow and approves the ADR schemes in Section 3 — doesn't resolve individual complaints itself.

When to use: To understand your rights and which ADR scheme (Section 3) your provider belongs to.

Want to know more? Read our full guide →

The Australian telecom, broadcasting, and anti-spam regulator

Australian Communications and Media Authority (ACMA) acma.gov.au →

What: Regulates telecom, broadcasting, and spam/robocall rules in Australia, including the Telecommunications Consumer Protections Code.

When to use: To understand the rules a telco must follow, or to report a scam call/text pattern at scale rather than an individual dispute (use the TIO in Section 3 for that).

Who coordinates telecom regulation across the EU

BEREC — Body of European Regulators for Electronic Communications berec.europa.eu →

What: BEREC coordinates the national telecom regulators across EU member states and shapes EU-wide policy — it does not resolve individual consumer complaints. Think of it as who sets the rules, not who to complain to.

When to use: To understand EU-level telecom policy direction. For an actual complaint, use your national regulator or the ECC-Net (Section 4).

Section 2 · Utility Regulators Directory

Energy and water regulators — distinct bodies overseeing distinct markets from the telecom regulators above. No EU-wide entry here: there is no single EU consumer-facing energy/water regulator, since each member state runs its own — this gap is intentional.

Who regulates US wholesale energy markets

Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) ferc.gov →

What: Regulates wholesale electricity/gas markets and interstate transmission. FERC does not regulate retail rates or handle individual consumer complaints — that's each state's own Public Utility Commission (PUC), below.

When to use: To understand wholesale market structure. For a retail billing or service complaint, use your state PUC (via NARUC, next) instead.

Find your state's utility regulator (PUC)

National Association of Regulatory Utility Commissioners (NARUC) naruc.org/resources/member-directory →

What: National association of state utility commissions — its member directory is the entry point for finding your specific state's Public Utility Commission, which regulates electricity, gas, and water rates and disconnection rules.

When to use: If your utility threatens disconnection unfairly, or you believe you were billed outside your state's approved rate schedule.

Want to know more? Read our full guide → Run the full checklist →

The UK energy regulator

Ofgem (UK) ofgem.gov.uk →

What: UK regulator for electricity and gas retail and wholesale markets — sets the rules suppliers must follow.

When to use: When your energy supplier is overcharging, failing to respond to complaints, or behaving unlawfully.

Want to know more? Read our full guide →

The UK water regulator

Ofwat (UK) ofwat.gov.uk →

What: UK regulator for water and sewerage services in England and Wales only — Scotland and Northern Ireland have separate arrangements.

When to use: For rules and standards governing your water company, if you're in England or Wales.

The Australian energy regulator

Australian Energy Regulator (AER) aer.gov.au →

What: Regulates wholesale and retail electricity and gas markets in most Australian states (Western Australia and the Northern Territory have separate arrangements).

When to use: To understand the rules your energy retailer must follow.

Section 3 · Ombudsman Directory

Complementary to Section 1's regulators: a regulator oversees the market, an ombudsman scheme resolves your individual dispute. No US entry here — there's no federal telecom/energy ombudsman in the US; US consumers use the complaint portals in Section 4 or their state PUC directly. This gap is intentional.

Resolve an individual UK telecom dispute (scheme 1 of 2)

Communications Ombudsman (UK) commsombudsman.org →

What: One of the two Ofcom-approved ADR schemes for telecom disputes. UK providers use one of these two schemes, not both — check your bill or provider's website to know which applies to you.

When to use: After your provider's own complaints process has failed and it's confirmed this is your provider's scheme.

Resolve an individual UK telecom dispute (scheme 2 of 2)

CISAS (UK) cedr-assist.com — CISAS overview →

What: The other Ofcom-approved ADR scheme for telecom disputes, operated by CEDR. As with the Communications Ombudsman above, your provider uses one scheme or the other — check which before you file.

When to use: After your provider's own complaints process has failed and it's confirmed this is your provider's scheme.

Resolve an individual UK energy (or some water) dispute

Energy Ombudsman (UK) energyombudsman.org →

What: Free, independent ombudsman for energy disputes (and some water complaints). Can award compensation and direct companies to take action.

When to use: After 8 weeks of an unresolved complaint with your supplier, or if they issue a deadlock letter.

Want to know more? Read our full guide →

Resolve an individual Australian phone/internet dispute

TIO — Telecommunications Industry Ombudsman (Australia) tio.com.au →

What: Free, independent dispute resolution for phone and internet complaints — can make binding decisions up to $100,000 AUD.

When to use: When your internet or mobile provider won't process a cancellation, is charging an unfair early termination fee, or hasn't delivered the promised service.

Want to know more? Read our full guide →

Resolve an individual Australian energy/water dispute (state-based)

EWON — Energy & Water Ombudsman NSW (Australia) ewon.com.au →

What: Energy and water ombudsman schemes in Australia are state-based, not national. EWON covers New South Wales and is listed here as the representative link — each state/territory has its own scheme: EWON (NSW), EWOV (Victoria), EWOQ (Queensland), EWOSA (South Australia), and others. Check which one covers your state before filing.

When to use: After your energy or water provider's own complaints process has failed, once you've confirmed the correct scheme for your state.

Section 4 · Consumer Complaint Portals

Where to actually file a complaint — as distinct from the regulators (Section 1-2, set the rules) and ombudsman schemes (Section 3, resolve disputes once your provider's own process has failed).

File a complaint with the FCC about your telecom provider

FCC — Consumer Complaint Center consumercomplaints.fcc.gov →

What: Official federal complaint portal for internet, phone, TV, and cable billing or service disputes. Your provider is legally required to respond in writing within 30 days.

When to use: If your ISP or phone provider won't resolve a billing error, service outage, or contract dispute through normal customer support.

Want to know more? Read our full guide → Generate your complaint letter →

Report telecom or utility fraud and bad business practices (US)

ReportFraud.ftc.gov (US) reportfraud.ftc.gov →

What: The FTC's portal for reporting fraud and bad business practices, including telecom/utility scams and billing issues outside the FCC's specific jurisdiction.

When to use: For a scam or deceptive practice, as distinct from a standard service/billing dispute (use the FCC portal above for that).

Get free advice before escalating a UK telecom or energy complaint

Citizens Advice consumer service (UK) citizensadvice.org.uk/consumer →

What: Free, independent advice on consumer rights — the recommended first step before escalating a telecom or energy complaint to an ombudsman (Section 3).

When to use: Before you escalate, to confirm you're taking the right first steps and have a genuine case.

Want to know more? Read our full guide →

File the actual complaint with the TIO (Australia)

TIO complaint form (Australia) tio.com.au/make-a-complaint →

What: The same TIO ombudsman as Section 3, linked here specifically as the direct complaint-filing action rather than the general overview.

When to use: Once you're ready to actually lodge a complaint, not just read about the process.

File a cross-border consumer complaint in the EU

ECC-Net — European Consumer Centres Network (EU) eccnet.eu — find your local ECC →

What: A network of national consumer centres (one per EU member state, plus Norway and Iceland) offering free help with cross-border disputes. This replaces the EU's Online Dispute Resolution (ODR) platform, which was discontinued 20 July 2025 — do not look for or trust any page still referencing the old ODR platform.

When to use: For a dispute with a telecom or utility provider based in a different EU country than yours.

Section 5 · Provider Cancellation Pages

Not all of these are self-service "cancel" buttons — some require a phone call or chat. The value here is having the correct page or number immediately rather than searching for it; treat each entry's actual process as described, not assumed.

Cancel Xfinity/Comcast service online

Xfinity/Comcast (US) xfinity.com/cancel →

What: A genuine self-service cancellation request page — no phone call required to start the process.

Cancel an AT&T line or service

AT&T Wireless (US) att.com — cancel a line or service →

What: Covers cancelling a wireless line or service. AT&T Internet/Fiber has no online cancellation option — that requires a phone call to 1-800-288-2020. Don't expect the same self-service flow across both product lines.

Change or cancel a BT package

BT (UK) bt.com — change or cancel a package →

What: Official guidance for changing or cancelling a BT phone, TV, or broadband package.

Cancel Sky broadband or TV

Sky (UK) sky.com — cancel Sky Broadband →

What: Sky's official help article covering cancellation within and outside the cooling-off period. TV cancellation has its own separate official article at sky.com/help/articles/cancel-sky-tv.

Cancel Virgin Media broadband or TV

Virgin Media (UK) virginmedia.com — leaving Virgin Media →

What: Official cancellation guidance — 30 days' notice, a 14-day cooling-off period on new contracts, and early disconnection fees outside your minimum term. No online self-service cancel form: you cancel via chat or by calling 150 (or 0345 454 1111).

Cancel a Telstra mobile or check your contract

Telstra (Australia) telstra.com.au — cancel a mobile service →

What: Official support for cancelling a mobile service. For checking contract terms or cancelling other services, see telstra.com.au — check contract or cancel service →.

Cancel or disconnect an Optus service

Optus (Australia) optus.com.au — contact us →

What: Optus has no self-service online cancellation form. From this contact page, select "Moving & Disconnecting" to message 24/7 support via My Account, or call 1300 555 241.
