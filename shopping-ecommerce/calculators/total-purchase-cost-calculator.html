Free Calculator

What's this actually going to cost you?

Online checkouts routinely show a low item price, then add shipping, tax, and — for international orders — customs duties only at the very end, or not until after you've already paid. Enter what you're seeing and get the real total before you commit.

Enter your local combined state + city/county rate — this varies by exact location in the US, so check your receipt or your state's revenue department site for the precise figure; this calculator can't look it up for you automatically.

The $800 duty-free exemption is gone

As of 2026, the $800 "de minimis" exemption that used to let low-value international purchases enter the US duty-free has been suspended for shipments from EVERY country — first for China and Hong Kong (May 2025), then for all other countries (August 2025), with the suspension continued indefinitely by CBP in June 2026. This means nearly every international online purchase, regardless of price, can now be subject to duties — including small, cheap items that used to ship duty-free. Congress has also scheduled the exemption's full legal elimination for July 2027, so this isn't expected to be reversed.

Actual duty rates vary enormously by the product's origin and category — as a rough guide, shipments from China/Hong Kong have faced notably higher rates than shipments from most other countries in 2026, but Kibbo can't calculate your exact rate for you. Check the marketplace/seller's checkout page (many now disclose an estimated duty at checkout) or your national customs authority for the number that applies to your specific item.

Duties are typically assessed on the item's value, not the shipping cost — this calculator applies your rate to the item price only.
Some carriers (postal services, couriers) charge a flat per-package fee for processing customs, separate from the duty itself. Leave at 0 if you don't know of one.

Please enter a valid item price before calculating.

Checking their math, not just their checkout total

Sellers in many places are legally required to disclose mandatory fees before you enter payment details — the US FTC's rules against "drip pricing," the UK's Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024, and the EU's Omnibus Directive all target exactly this. If the total this calculator gives you is noticeably higher than what actually showed up at checkout, something wasn't disclosed the way it should have been.

Run a checkout you're suspicious of through Kibbo's Hidden Fees Checker checklist to spot the pattern before you pay, not after.
