How to make a claim

Notify DPD in writing; DPD's own T&Cs (Condition 11 'Liability for loss and damage', Condition 12 'Extended Cover', Condition 13 'Claims for compensation').

Official claim form
https://www.dpd.co.uk/content/terms-and-conditions.jsp states only that the customer must notify 'the Company' within 14 days — it does not itself give a submission address, email, or form URL. We could not locate a separate, working DPD UK contact/claims page this session.
Claims email
Not stated in our data — check DPD's official page directly: https://www.dpd.co.uk/content/terms-and-conditions.jsp
Postal address
DPDgroup UK Ltd, Roebuck Lane, Smethwick, B66 1BY, United Kingdom (registered office, confirmed in the footer of dpd.co.uk's own homepage; this is DPD's only officially published postal address — not a separate claims-department address, since DPD does not publish one)

Evidence to gather

  • Order confirmation or receipt
  • Tracking number and tracking history (a screenshot, with dates/status)
  • Proof of the item's value
  • Photos of the item and packaging, if it arrived damaged
  • Any correspondence with the seller or DPD so far
  • Delivery Evidence Log template — a structured way to keep all of this in one place

What happens next

No ADR/ombudsman scheme found for DPD specifically (not a POSTRS member). Escalation realistically runs via the retailer who chose DPD.

UK note: POSTRS (the Postal Redress Service) covers Royal Mail only. It does not cover DPD. For a non-Royal-Mail UK carrier, the realistic escalation route is via the retailer who chose the carrier, or a card dispute (see below) — never assume ADR membership that isn't confirmed.

United Kingdom

Section 75 Consumer Credit Act 1974 (credit card, £100-£30,000 single item) or chargeback (any card, under the card scheme's own rules)

Consumer Credit Act 1974, s.75: the credit card issuer is jointly and severally liable with the supplier for breach of contract/misrepresentation, for a single item priced over £100 and not more than £30,000. For debit cards, or credit card purchases outside that range, the only route is a chargeback under Visa/Mastercard/Amex's own scheme rules (not a statutory right).
