Arriving at a hotel booked through Booking.com only to be told there's no room due to overbooking feels like exactly the kind of situation you're left to sort out alone. You're not entirely unprotected — but it matters whether your booking was a standalone hotel reservation or part of a bundled package, because the strength of your legal position differs between the two.

## If your hotel was part of a package — the strongest protection

If you booked flight and hotel together, or were redirected to book a linked service within 24 hours of an initial booking, this typically qualifies as a "package" or "linked travel arrangement" under Directive (EU) 2015/2302. In that case, the organiser has a clearly codified obligation to provide alternative accommodation of equivalent or higher quality at no extra cost, plus necessary transport to the new location.

## If it was a standalone hotel booking — a different, more general right

Booking just a hotel room through Booking.com, with nothing else bundled in, doesn't trigger the Package Travel Directive's specific relocation mandate. Your remedy here comes from general consumer contract principles instead: the hotel has failed to perform the service it was contracted for with reasonable care, which is a breach of contract you can pursue directly against the hotel (your actual contracting party in most cases), rather than a specific EU "overbooking compensation" regulation equivalent to the one covering flights.

## Price transparency — this part applies regardless of booking type

EU rules strictly prohibit dark patterns and hidden costs in either scenario. The price initially displayed must include all unavoidable fees, service charges, and fixed supplements. Local tourist taxes charged by municipalities, where they can't be collected online, must be clearly broken out and quantified from the first step of the booking process — no surprise charges at reception.

## What to do on the spot

If you experience overbooking or the accommodation doesn't match what was booked, take photos immediately, request an official complaints form at the property, and contact Booking's urgent support simultaneously — for package/linked bookings, explicitly request relocation under your package travel rights; for standalone bookings, frame it as the hotel's failure to perform the contracted service.

## Escalating unresolved disputes

If the intermediary or hotel won't cover the extraordinary costs caused by their service failure, EU residents can pursue the case through their national European Consumer Centre (ECC-Net) — a free public body specialising in resolving cross-border tourism disputes without going to court.
