Can a courier leave my parcel outside without my permission?

A courier's convenience isn't the same thing as your consent — and the difference matters if the parcel disappears.

Kibbo Consumer Desk · Updated September 2026 · 5 min read

Not without consequences landing back on the seller if it goes wrong. If you never gave delivery instructions authorizing a "safe place," and a courier decides on their own to leave your parcel on a doorstep, behind a bin, or with a stranger, the seller remains fully responsible if it's then lost, stolen, or damaged. This is a direct extension of the same principle covering missing "delivered" parcels: your contract is with the seller, and they can't shift responsibility onto a courier decision you had no part in.

What the law says

United States United Kingdom
Legal basis General principles under the FTC Mail Order Rule and state consumer protection law — the seller's delivery obligation isn't satisfied by an unauthorized drop-off Consumer Rights Act 2015 — the seller is responsible until goods reach the consumer or an agreed alternative safe place
Authorized safe place If you specifically instructed a safe location, responsibility for what happens there generally shifts more toward you as the customer Explicitly recognized — an agreed safe place satisfies the delivery obligation, even if the item is later lost from there
Unauthorized safe place The seller's delivery obligation isn't met by leaving it somewhere you didn't agree to The seller remains responsible if it's left somewhere you never authorized and it goes missing or is damaged
Does a delivery photo change this? A photo shows the courier's action, not that the delivery was properly completed under the seller's obligation Same — a photo alone doesn't establish that an agreed safe place was used, or that the item was securely left

Worth being precise about the UK example specifically cited by consumer guidance: if Royal Mail (or any courier) leaves a parcel with a neighbor or in a location you told them to use, it's not the seller's or courier's responsibility if it later goes missing from there. But if they choose that location themselves, without your instruction, responsibility stays with the seller.

Where this actually goes wrong

A courier leaving a parcel in plain view of the street

Without your authorization, this is squarely the courier/seller's risk, not yours — even if the parcel photo shows it was technically "delivered" to your address.

"Left with a neighbour" you don't actually know

If this wasn't a neighbor you specified, this doesn't count as an agreed safe place, regardless of how reasonable the courier thought the choice was.

The seller pointing to the delivery photo as proof of nothing wrong

A photo confirms the courier's action at that moment — it doesn't confirm the parcel stayed safe afterward, or that leaving it there was ever something you agreed to.

What to say

"I did not authorize my parcel to be left [outside / with a neighbour / in any alternative location]. As this wasn't an agreed safe place, I'd like this treated as a non-delivery and request a redelivery or full refund."

If the seller points to a delivery photo as evidence of completion:

"The photo shows the courier's action, but I never gave instructions authorizing this location. Under [the Consumer Rights Act 2015 / your delivery obligations], this doesn't satisfy delivery to me or an agreed safe place."

If it isn't resolved

  1. Check your order history to confirm whether any safe-place instruction was actually given by you.
  2. Request the redelivery or refund in writing, citing the lack of authorization clearly.
  3. If your card offers purchase protection, a chargeback or Section 75 claim (UK, £100-£30,000) is a direct route to recovering your money.
  4. Escalate to the Retail Ombudsman (UK) or file an FTC complaint (US) if the seller refuses to acknowledge the issue.
