Flights & Travel · US & UK

Can I get my money back if my flight is delayed for hours?

Two completely different systems: one pays you to walk away, the other pays you even if you still get on the plane.

Kibbo Consumer Desk·Updated August 2026·7 min read
Short answer

In the US, a long enough delay gives you the right to an automatic cash refund — but only if you choose not to travel. If you still take the delayed flight, there's no fixed cash payout, just care (meals, hotel) if the airline caused the delay. In the UK, it works the other way around: you can get fixed cash compensation of £220-£520 for the delay itself, whether or not you still flew, as long as the airline was at fault. Knowing which system you're under changes your entire strategy the moment you're standing at the gate.

What the law says

United StatesUnited Kingdom
TriggerDomestic delay of 3+ hours, international 6+ hours, measured against the original scheduled timeArrival delay of 3+ hours at final destination
If you don't flyAutomatic cash refund to original payment method — the airline can't force a voucher on youRefund or re-routing under Article 8, your choice
If you do flyNo fixed cash compensation — DOT considered a compensation rule in 2024 and withdrew it in November 2025£220–£520 cash compensation regardless of whether you still flew, unless "extraordinary circumstances" apply
Care while delayedMeal after a roughly 3-hour wait if the cause was within the airline's control; hotel + transport for overnight delaysMeals, hotel, transport — not conditional on cause, applies regardless of fault
Refund timingCredit card: 7 business days. Cash/other: 20 calendar daysImmediate for compensation; refund processed promptly under Article 8

This is the single biggest transatlantic difference in air passenger rights, and it trips up a lot of travelers who assume "delay" means the same protections everywhere. The US model protects your right to walk away with your money if a delay is bad enough. The UK/EU model compensates you for the inconvenience of the delay itself, independent of whether you completed the trip.

One more wrinkle worth knowing: as of late 2025 the DOT paused enforcement of one narrow piece of its refund rule — when an airline purely relabels a flight number without any real change to your itinerary — through mid-2027 while it redrafts that specific definition. This doesn't touch genuine delays or cancellations; it only affects pure flight-number renumbering with nothing else different about your trip.

Your situation — which one is this?

US, deciding not to fly Your domestic flight is delayed 3+ hours and you don't want to wait it out

You have the right to decline the rebooking and demand a full cash refund to your original payment method — not a voucher, not a travel credit, unless you specifically choose one. This applies regardless of why the flight was delayed.

US, choosing to fly You wait it out and take the delayed flight

No fixed cash compensation is owed for the wait itself. If the delay was within the airline's control (not weather), you're entitled to meals and, for an overnight delay, a hotel and transport — but this is a service obligation, not a cash payment.

UK, either way Your flight arrives 3+ hours late at your destination

You're owed £220-£520 in cash compensation regardless of whether you completed the trip or not, unless the airline can show a genuine extraordinary circumstance. This is a fixed entitlement, not a discretionary gesture.

Watch for this Being pushed toward a voucher before you've asked about your refund rights

At a busy gate during a mass delay, staff sometimes default to offering meal or hotel vouchers as if that's the full extent of what's owed — without mentioning the separate cash refund right (US) or compensation right (UK) that may also apply. Vouchers for care and your refund/compensation entitlement are two different things; accepting one doesn't waive the other unless you're told otherwise in writing.

Where airlines get it wrong

Legitimate

"Your flight is delayed 4 hours. If you'd like, we can rebook you, or you can decline and receive a full refund to your original payment method within the standard processing window."

Overreach

Offering only a travel credit or voucher as "the resolution," without mentioning that a cash refund is your legal right if you decline to travel (US) or that compensation is owed independent of rebooking (UK).

What to say

US — declining to travel

You, at the gate or via chat "My flight [number] is delayed [X hours] beyond the DOT significant-delay threshold. I'm declining the rebooking and requesting my automatic cash refund to my original form of payment, per the DOT refund rule."

UK — requesting compensation

You, filing the claim "My flight [number] arrived [X hours] late at [destination]. Under UK261 I'm entitled to £[amount] compensation, in addition to any refund or care already provided. Please confirm payment within a reasonable timeframe."

If they refuse

  1. Keep your documentation. Boarding pass, rebooking emails, and the actual arrival time — a flight-tracker screenshot (Flightradar24 or FlightAware) is strong evidence if the airline disputes the delay length.
  2. US: file a complaint with DOT Aviation Consumer Protection if the airline won't issue the refund.
  3. UK: escalate to an approved ADR body or the CAA if the airline denies compensation without a genuine extraordinary-circumstances reason.
