Can a crypto app freeze my money without warning?

"We can't discuss the details of your account" isn't the platform being difficult — it's often the law forcing them into silence.

Kibbo Consumer Desk·Updated August 2026·7 min read
Short answer

Yes, and this is one of the more frustrating realities of crypto and fintech accounts in both countries: a platform can restrict your account instantly the moment automated systems or a compliance officer flags unusual activity, and it often legally cannot tell you the specific reason. This isn't a customer-service failure — anti-money-laundering "tipping-off" rules genuinely prohibit a firm from confirming that a Suspicious Activity Report has been filed. Your money isn't gone, and a freeze isn't the same as a confiscation, but the silence you get from support is frequently a legal requirement, not evasiveness.

What the law says

United StatesUnited Kingdom
Can they freeze without warning?Yes — automated AML systems or compliance review can trigger an instant restriction, typically reported to FinCEN if suspiciousYes — reports in these cases go to the National Crime Agency (NCA)
Must they explain why?No — if tied to a Suspicious Activity Report, federal law prohibits disclosure ("tipping off")No — the same tipping-off principle applies; if the bank submits a Defence Against Money Laundering (DAML) request, a statutory 7-working-day notice period applies, followed by a 31-day moratorium if consent is refused
Common triggersLarge or unusual transactions, inconsistent deposit patterns, international transfers without clear purpose, mismatched account activity vs. stated profileSame general triggers, plus enforcement mechanisms like Account Freezing Orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002 for suspected recoverable property above £3,000 (raised from £1,000 in June 2025)
Can you still receive money?Generally yes — a frozen account typically still accepts incoming funds; it's withdrawals and transfers that stopSame principle applies
How long can it last?Varies widely — a fraud hold often clears in days; an AML investigation or legal order can take weeks to monthsAn Account Freezing Order can last up to two years, potentially followed by forfeiture proceedings you can contest

It's worth being precise about the distinction between a freeze and a closure: a frozen account is not confiscated, and your money remains legally yours — you've simply lost access while the platform, or in more serious cases a law enforcement agency, examines something. Each type of freeze has a different legal basis and timeline, and the platform genuinely may not know (or be allowed to say) which type applies to your specific case.

Your situation — which one is this?

Most common, resolves fastest An automated flag on a single unusual transaction

A large or atypical transfer that doesn't match your account's history is the single most frequent trigger. This is often resolved in days once you provide documentation — an invoice, a clear payment reference, or proof of the source of funds.

Watch for this Being told "there's nothing we can do" with zero further detail, repeatedly

If every support interaction gives you the identical scripted non-answer, that's often the tell that a Suspicious Activity Report has genuinely been filed and staff are legally constrained — not that they're simply unhelpful. Understanding this doesn't unfreeze the account faster, but it changes what you should actually push for (documentation and process, not "the real reason").

More serious A formal legal freezing order

In the UK specifically, an Account Freezing Order under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002 is a court-authorized action, often obtained early in an investigation — sometimes before you're even aware you're being looked into. This is a different and more serious category than a routine compliance hold, and generally warrants qualified legal advice.

Preventable Irregular deposit patterns or mixed personal/crypto business activity

Frequent large transfers, inconsistent deposits, or crypto-related business activity run through a personal account are common triggers precisely because they don't match a "typical" profile that automated systems are tuned to expect — even when every transaction is entirely legitimate.

Where the frustration is legitimate — and where it isn't

Legally constrained (not the platform's fault)

Support staff genuinely cannot confirm or deny that a Suspicious Activity Report has been filed — this is a criminal offense for them to disclose in both countries if a report exists, regardless of how frustrating the silence feels to you.

A legitimate complaint

A platform that provides no process at all for submitting documentation, gives no realistic timeline, or fails to release essential funds for reasonable living expenses when legally able to — these are fair grounds for escalation, separate from the underlying freeze itself.

What to do while frozen

Providing documentation proactively

You, to the platform's compliance team "I understand my account has been restricted. I'd like to provide documentation to support a review — [invoices/proof of source of funds/transaction context]. Please let me know what additional information would help resolve this."

Requesting essential funds during a freeze

You, if the freeze is extended "Given the length of this restriction, I'd like to request access to a limited amount for essential living expenses, if that's something you're able to accommodate during your review."

If the freeze drags on

  1. Respond promptly and completely to every documentation request — incomplete responses are one of the most common reasons freezes extend unnecessarily.
  2. Never attempt to move funds through unofficial channels while frozen — this can be interpreted as an attempt to circumvent the freeze and worsen your position.
  3. Keep a written record of every interaction, including dates and names of who you spoke with.
  4. UK: if this escalates into a formal Account Freezing Order, seek qualified legal counsel — there may be grounds to apply to vary or set aside the order.
  5. Both countries: if the freeze seems disproportionate or unresolved for an unreasonable period, escalate to the platform's formal complaints process, then to the relevant financial ombudsman or regulator.
