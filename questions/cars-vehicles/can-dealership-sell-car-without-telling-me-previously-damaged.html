Can a dealership sell me a car without telling me it was previously damaged?

No — and dealers have been fined and even jailed for doing exactly this.

Kibbo Editorial Team · Updated September 2026 · 7 min read

No. If a dealer knows a car has been damaged and repaired and doesn't tell you, that's a misleading omission under the Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008 (CPUTR) — enforceable through both civil and criminal courts. Crucially, the duty to disclose isn't triggered only when you ask the right question: the onus is on the trader to volunteer this information, whether or not you specifically request it.

This isn't a grey area regulators overlook — Trading Standards has actively prosecuted dealers for exactly this, and consumers have successfully pursued both criminal complaints and civil redress from the same set of facts.

Why "it was fixed properly" doesn't excuse non-disclosure

A common dealer defence is that the damage was repaired to a high standard — sometimes manufacturer-approved — so there's nothing left to disclose. This doesn't hold up: in a documented real case, a dealer made exactly this argument after a MINI was repaired twice for accident damage before being sold undisclosed, and the guidance confirmed the dealer had still committed an offence. Quality of repair is a separate question from whether the buyer was entitled to know about the history at all.

Your two separate legal angles

  • Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008: a misleading omission gives you a right of redress if the missing information was a significant factor in your decision to buy — this is squarely what withheld accident history usually is.
  • Consumer Rights Act 2015: if the undisclosed damage means the car isn't of satisfactory quality or doesn't match its description, you may also have a standard rejection or repair claim, separate from the misrepresentation angle.

These aren't mutually exclusive — raising both gives you a stronger, more complete case than relying on just one.

What to do if you discover undisclosed damage

  1. Get independent confirmation of the damage and repair history — a body shop report, an HPI-style vehicle history check, or documentation from wherever the repair was actually carried out.
  2. Write to the dealer setting out what you've discovered, citing the Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008 and, if relevant, the Consumer Rights Act 2015.
  3. State clearly what you're asking for — a refund, a price reduction, or the dealer taking the car back — and that you wouldn't have bought it, or paid the same price, had you known.
  4. Report the trader to Trading Standards, particularly if the dealer denies wrongdoing or refuses to engage.
  5. If unresolved, pursue civil redress through the small claims court, or via a finance provider's dispute process if you bought on finance.

More on this

What can I do if I find out my car had undisclosed accident damage? You may have grounds for redress under the Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008, alongside a claim under the Consumer Rights Act 2015 if the undisclosed damage means the car isn't of satisfactory quality or as described. You can also report the trader to Trading Standards, which has prosecuted dealers for exactly this offence.

Does repairing damage to a manufacturer-approved standard remove the duty to disclose it? No. Dealers have argued that repairing damage to an approved standard removes any obligation to disclose it, but this isn't a recognised defence — a documented case involving exactly this argument confirmed the dealer had still committed an offence by not disclosing the prior accident and repair.
