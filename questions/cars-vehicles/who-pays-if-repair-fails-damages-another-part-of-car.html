Who pays if a repair fails and damages another part of the car?

Reasonable care and skill isn't optional — if a botched repair causes new damage, that's usually the garage's bill to pay.

Kibbo Consumer Desk · Updated September 2026 · 6 min read

Normally, the garage is liable. Under UK consumer law, anyone providing a service — including a car repair — must carry out that work with reasonable care and skill. If a poorly executed repair leads directly to new damage elsewhere in the vehicle, that's a breach of that duty. The garage is generally responsible for putting the resulting damage right, not just re-doing the original job you paid for.

Establishing the link between the repair and the new fault

ScenarioLikely outcome
New fault appears shortly after the repair, in a directly related systemStrong case the garage is liable — e.g. a poorly fitted part causing a related failure
Garage disputes the connectionAn independent inspection from an uninvolved mechanic can establish the causal link
New fault is genuinely unrelated (separate, pre-existing issue)Garage isn't liable for that unrelated problem
Garage refuses to engage at allFormal written complaint, then small claims court if unresolved

What evidence strengthens your case

  • The original invoice describing exactly what work was carried out.
  • A clear timeline: when the repair was done, and when the new fault first appeared.
  • Photos or diagnostic printouts showing the new damage.
  • An independent mechanic's report, if the garage disputes the connection — this is often the deciding piece of evidence.

What you can claim

You can generally claim the reasonable cost of repairing the new damage caused by the substandard work. In some cases, you may also be able to claim related losses — such as recovery costs or the cost of reasonable alternative transport — if the fault left the car undriveable. Keep every receipt and quote, and put your claim to the garage in writing first, giving them a reasonable opportunity to respond before escalating.

Steps to take

  1. Get an independent assessment if the garage disputes that the new fault is connected to their work.
  2. Write to the garage explaining the issue, referencing the original invoice and the independent report if you have one.
  3. Request the cost of the repair, plus any directly related losses.
  4. If the garage refuses to resolve it, you can pursue a small claims court case — most disputes of this kind fall well within the small claims limit.
