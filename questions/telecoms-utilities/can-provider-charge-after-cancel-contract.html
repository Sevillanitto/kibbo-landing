Can my provider charge me after I cancel my contract?

The billing-cycle-in-progress explanation is often legitimate. A brand new charge after that isn't.

Kibbo Consumer Desk · Updated September 2026 · 6 min read

No — once a cancellation has genuinely taken effect, any further charge is a billing error, not a legitimate continuation of your service. The confusing part in practice is that many providers bill in advance, so your last legitimate charge might cover a period extending slightly past the date you actually called to cancel — that's usually normal and not the same as being charged for entirely new billing cycles after cancellation was confirmed.

The distinction that actually matters

What happened Legitimate?
You cancel mid-cycle, and your final bill covers through the end of that already-paid billing period Usually yes — this reflects how advance billing works, not an unauthorized new charge
You're charged for a completely new billing cycle that starts AFTER your confirmed cancellation date No — this is a billing error
You were never given a clear cancellation confirmation, and charges simply continued No — and this is also worth investigating whether the cancellation request was processed at all
An early termination fee applies because you cancelled before your contract term ended This is a different, separate issue — check whether such a fee was disclosed when you signed up, not the same as ordinary service billing continuing

How to actually get this resolved

  1. Locate your cancellation confirmation — a confirmation number, email, or chat transcript with a specific date service would end.
  2. Compare that date against your billing statement — identify exactly which charges fall after it.
  3. Contact the provider directly with this evidence and request a refund of everything charged after the confirmed end date.
  4. If they don't resolve it, or you never received clear confirmation in the first place, dispute the charge with your bank or card issuer as a billing error — in the US, the Fair Credit Billing Act gives you 60 days from the statement date for credit cards.
  5. In the UK, if unresolved directly with the provider, escalate to the relevant Alternative Dispute Resolution scheme (Ombudsman Services: Communications or CISAS, depending on which one your provider is signed up to).
