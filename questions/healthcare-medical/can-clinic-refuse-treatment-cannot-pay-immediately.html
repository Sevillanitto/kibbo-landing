Healthcare & Medical · US & UK

Can a clinic refuse treatment because I cannot pay immediately?

The word doing all the legal work here is "emergency" — it draws a hard line between what a provider must do and what it can choose to do.

Kibbo Consumer Desk · Updated September 2026 · 6 min read

Not for a genuine emergency — that much is settled law in both countries. In the US, the Emergency Medical Treatment and Labor Act (EMTALA) requires any hospital emergency department that accepts Medicare funding — which is nearly all of them — to provide a medical screening exam and stabilizing treatment regardless of your ability to pay. In the UK, NHS emergency care is free at the point of use for everyone, full stop. Where the picture genuinely changes is non-emergency, private, or elective care: outside a true emergency, a clinic generally can decline to proceed without payment or an agreed payment arrangement.

What the law says

United States United Kingdom
Legal basis — emergency care EMTALA (Emergency Medical Treatment and Labor Act) NHS founding principle — care free at the point of use, funded through general taxation
What's required A medical screening exam and stabilizing treatment for anyone presenting at a covered emergency department, regardless of insurance status or ability to pay Emergency (A&E) treatment is provided to everyone present in the UK, regardless of ability to pay or immigration status, in most circumstances
What counts as an emergency An acute medical condition where the absence of immediate attention could reasonably be expected to place your health in serious jeopardy Broadly similar — genuine urgent/emergency presentations, as opposed to routine or elective care
Non-emergency / elective care Not covered by EMTALA — a private provider or elective procedure can generally require payment or insurance verification before proceeding Non-emergency NHS care remains free for eligible patients; private non-emergency clinics can decline to proceed without payment, same as the US
Can you be billed later for emergency care? Yes — EMTALA guarantees the treatment itself, not that it's free; a bill can still follow afterward, subject to the No Surprises Act's separate protections NHS emergency care doesn't generate a bill to the patient in the way US emergency care might

It's worth being precise about what EMTALA actually guarantees: the treatment, not a waiver of the eventual bill. A US emergency room can't turn you away or delay stabilizing care over payment, but you can still receive a bill afterward for that care — this is a separate issue from the No Surprises Act protections around surprise billing.

Where this actually goes wrong

A genuine emergency treated as routine

If a facility is asking about payment or insurance before providing a medical screening exam for what's genuinely an emergency presentation, that's a direct EMTALA issue in the US — the screening exam has to happen first, regardless of payment discussion.

Non-emergency care being confused with emergency protections

A private clinic declining to schedule an elective procedure without payment isn't a violation of anything — this is the situation where a clinic's normal payment terms genuinely apply, and it's worth distinguishing this clearly from an emergency scenario.

Being transferred or discharged before you're actually stabilized

EMTALA specifically covers the point at which a patient is stable enough to be discharged or transferred — being moved along before that point, for reasons connected to payment, is a compliance issue worth documenting.

What to say

If you believe emergency treatment is being delayed over payment in the US:

"I understand under EMTALA I'm entitled to a medical screening exam and stabilizing treatment regardless of my ability to pay. Can we address the medical evaluation first?"

If you're arranging non-emergency care and need a payment plan:

"I'm not able to pay the full amount upfront — can we discuss a payment plan or installment arrangement before scheduling this?"

If it isn't resolved

  1. Document the time, date, and details of any delay connected to payment discussion during what you believe was an emergency.
  2. In the US, EMTALA violations can be reported to the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS).
  3. In the UK, if NHS emergency care is being withheld inappropriately, raise it with the hospital's PALS (Patient Advice and Liaison Service) team.
  4. For non-emergency care payment disputes, work directly with the clinic's billing department on a payment plan — this is a negotiation, not typically a legal violation.
