Can my landlord refuse to give me a receipt for rent?

A receipt costs the landlord nothing to provide, and protects you in exactly the situations where you'll need it most: disputes, benefits, and visa paperwork.

Kibbo Consumer Desk · Updated September 2026 · 5 min read

Not entirely, though the exact legal requirement depends on where you rent and how you pay. There's no single blanket federal or UK-wide law forcing a landlord to issue a receipt for every single rent payment, but a landlord flatly refusing to provide any proof of payment at all — especially for cash payments, and especially once you've asked in writing — is on weak ground in both countries. Several US states specifically require a receipt on request, and refusing one when reasonably asked tends to raise more questions about why than it answers.

What the law says

United States United Kingdom
Legal basis Varies by state — several states have specific statutes requiring a rent receipt, particularly for cash payments No single statute requiring a receipt for every payment, but landlords are expected to provide reasonable proof of payment on request as part of standard record-keeping obligations
Cash payments Many states specifically require a receipt for cash rent payments, given the lack of any other automatic record Best practice strongly recommended, and reasonable for a tenant to insist on given cash leaves no automatic trail
Electronic/bank transfer payments Less commonly mandated by statute, since the transfer itself creates a bank record Same reasoning — a bank transfer with a clear reference largely serves as its own proof
What a written request changes Some states specifically condition the requirement on the tenant having requested a receipt A written request creates a clear record that you asked and were refused, strengthening your position if a dispute follows

The practical takeaway that applies almost everywhere: if you're paying in cash, get in the habit of asking for a receipt every time, in writing if needed. If you're paying by bank transfer, keep your own bank statements as a backup — they function as proof of payment even without a formal receipt from the landlord.

Where this actually goes wrong

Cash payments with no record at all

This is the highest-risk scenario — if a dispute later arises over whether rent was paid on time, or paid at all, cash with no receipt leaves you with essentially nothing to point to.

A written request for a receipt going unanswered

Once you've specifically asked in writing, continued refusal is much harder for a landlord to justify as an oversight, and worth escalating rather than repeating the request indefinitely.

Needing proof of payment for an external purpose

Benefits applications, visa renewals, and some tax situations often specifically require documented proof of rent payment — if your landlord won't provide it, your own bank records become the fallback evidence.

What to say

"Could you provide a receipt for my rent payment of [amount] on [date]? I'd like to keep a record for my own files going forward."

If a request has already gone unanswered:

"I requested a receipt for my payment on [date] and haven't received one. Could you confirm you'll provide receipts for future payments, ideally by email so there's a clear record?"

If it isn't resolved

  1. Switch to a payment method that automatically generates its own proof — a bank transfer with a clear reference, rather than cash.
  2. Keep your own records regardless: bank statements, screenshots, or a simple payment log with dates and amounts.
  3. If your state has a specific receipt requirement, cite it directly when requesting.
  4. For an ongoing pattern of refusal, a local tenant rights organization can advise on your specific state or local requirements.
