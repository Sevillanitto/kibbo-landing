When Is Luggage Officially Considered Lost? Timelines and Statutory Rights

"Delayed" and "lost" aren't the same status, and the difference isn't just semantic — it determines whether you're entitled to buy a toothbrush on the airline's dime, or claim the full value of everything in the bag.

The Myth

"If my baggage doesn't arrive on the carousel after my flight, it's immediately classified as lost, and I can file a full loss claim on the spot."

This assumption leads a lot of travelers to submit the wrong type of claim on day one — and get understandably frustrated when the airline treats it as a delay case instead.

The Reality

Airlines operate under a specific legal distinction between "delayed" baggage and "lost" baggage. During an initial waiting window, your bag is classified as temporarily misplaced or still in transit — not lost. Under Article 17 and Article 31 of the Montreal Convention, along with DOT operational guidance, the airline has a defined period to locate and deliver your property before you're entitled to file a claim for total loss.

The Hard Data: The 21-Day Legal Boundary

Days 1 to 20: Delayed Baggage Status

During this window, your bag is classified as "in transit" or "temporarily misplaced," not lost. You're entitled to reimbursement for reasonable emergency essential expenses — toiletries, basic replacement clothing — against submitted receipts, but not for the full value of the bag's contents, since it hasn't yet been declared lost.

Day 21+: Officially "Lost" Baggage Status

Once 21 consecutive days pass from the date the bag was scheduled to arrive without it being located and returned to you, it's legally classified as permanently lost under international law. At that point, your entitlement converts from reimbursing emergency expenses to full content valuation reimbursement, up to the applicable statutory liability cap (domestic DOT or Montreal Convention SDR limit, depending on your itinerary).

There's one important exception to the 21-day wait: if the airline acknowledges in writing before Day 21 that the bag cannot be located — for instance, if it was confirmed destroyed or is genuinely untraceable — the bag is immediately declared lost at that point, and you don't need to wait out the remaining days to file your full claim.

Real Cases: Rights Transition at the 21-Day Mark

Case 1: Essential Expenses During the Delayed Phase

The scenario: Your suitcase doesn't arrive on a trip to a winter destination. You spend $400 on winter clothing and personal hygiene essentials while waiting for the airline to locate it.

The outcome: The airline locates and delivers your bag on Day 12. Because it arrived before Day 21, you can't claim the total value of the bag's contents — but the airline must still reimburse the $400 you spent on reasonable essential items, against valid receipts.

Case 2: Conversion to Total Loss

The scenario: The airline tracks your bag through WorldTracer for 21 days without success. On Day 22, it's still unlocated.

The outcome: The bag is officially declared lost. You're now entitled to claim the total depreciated value of the suitcase itself, plus everything inside it, up to the applicable cap — $4,700 for domestic U.S. flights, or 1,519 SDR for international flights.

The Protocol: Escalating After 21 Days

  1. Obtain written status escalation. Request written confirmation from the baggage service department that your file has moved from "Tracing" to "Total Loss."
  2. Compile a master loss inventory. List every item that was inside the suitcase — purchase dates, estimated original costs, and replacement values.
  3. Attach substantiating evidence. Your original ticket confirmation, baggage tag stub, copy of the initial PIR, and purchase receipts.
  4. Submit the formal lost baggage claim. File the complete packet with the airline's risk management or central baggage claims department within the statutory deadline for your jurisdiction.

If you're still inside the 21-day window, don't wait passively — keep every receipt for essential purchases along the way, since those are reimbursable regardless of whether the bag eventually turns up.
