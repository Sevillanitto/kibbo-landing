Protecting Your Deposit Policy From No-Show Chargebacks

Card networks side with restaurants far more often when the cancellation policy was clear, disclosed before booking, and backed by proper card authentication — not after a strongly worded email.

Why Chargebacks Succeed Against Restaurants

When a customer disputes a no-show deposit charge with their card issuer, the bank's decision usually turns on documentary evidence, not on who was actually "right." Card networks (Visa, Mastercard, and others) require the merchant to prove the customer clearly agreed to the specific charge terms before the transaction — if a restaurant can't produce that proof in the dispute response window, the chargeback is typically decided in the customer's favor by default, regardless of the underlying facts of the no-show itself.

What Actually Survives a Chargeback Dispute

  • Explicit, itemized disclosure at the time of booking — the cancellation policy, deposit amount, and the exact circumstances under which it's non-refundable must have been shown to the customer before they confirmed the reservation, not buried in a general terms link they weren't required to open.
  • Active acknowledgment, not passive disclosure — a checkbox or explicit confirmation step the customer had to actively complete carries far more weight in a dispute than a policy simply printed somewhere on the booking page.
  • 3D Secure (or equivalent) authentication at the time of card capture — in the EU and UK, Strong Customer Authentication under PSD2 provides a liability shift that generally favors the merchant when the transaction was properly authenticated, since the cardholder's bank itself verified the transaction at the time.
  • A timestamped confirmation record — email or SMS confirmation sent immediately after booking, restating the exact policy the customer agreed to, creates an independent record beyond the booking platform's own database.

Writing a Chargeback-Resistant Cancellation Policy

  1. State the exact deposit amount, the exact cancellation deadline, and the exact consequence of a no-show — vague language like "cancellation fees may apply" is far weaker than a specific figure and timeframe.
  2. Require active confirmation (a checkbox, a typed acknowledgment) at the point of booking, not a passive link to a separate policy page.
  3. Use a payment gateway that supports 3D Secure or your region's equivalent strong authentication standard, and confirm it's actually being applied to deposit-taking transactions specifically, not just standard payments.
  4. Send an automatic confirmation message immediately after booking that restates the policy in plain language — this becomes independent evidence in a dispute.
  5. Respond to any chargeback within the card network's stated window with the full evidence package (booking record, acknowledgment, confirmation message, authentication result) — a late or incomplete response is treated as a forfeit regardless of how strong the underlying evidence would have been.
