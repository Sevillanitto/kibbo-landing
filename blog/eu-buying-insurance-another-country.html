Buying Insurance in Another EU Country: What Should You Check?

EU passporting rules let insurers sell across borders — but the policy still needs checking as carefully as one bought at home, maybe more so.

Coverage and territorial scope

Confirm exactly which countries the policy provides cover in — a policy issued in one EU country doesn't automatically mean full coverage everywhere else in the EU. Territorial scope is a specific clause worth reading directly, not assuming from the insurer's general marketing.

Exclusions and limits

Check exclusions and coverage limits the same way you would for a domestic policy — these don't become more generous just because the insurer operates cross-border. Read the actual policy document, not a translated marketing summary.

Deductibles/excess

Confirm the deductible or excess structure and currency it's stated in — cross-border policies sometimes state amounts in a currency different from your own, which matters when estimating what you'd actually pay toward a claim.

Cancellation terms and language

Check the cancellation notice period and process, and confirm which language governs the contract in case of a dispute — this matters if you ever need to correspond formally with the insurer or a national redress body.

The claims process itself

Confirm how and where claims are actually handled — some cross-border insurers route claims through a local branch or partner, others require contact with an office in a different country. Ask specifically which national out-of-court redress body would apply if a dispute arose, since this depends on how the insurer is structured, not just where you live.
