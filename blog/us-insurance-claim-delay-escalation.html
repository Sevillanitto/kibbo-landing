Your Insurance Company Is Taking Too Long: When Should You Escalate a Claim?

Some delays are routine. Others cross into "unfair claims practices" territory that state regulators actually care about. Knowing the difference changes what you do next.

First, document instead of just feeling frustrated

Log every contact — date, method, who you spoke with, and exactly what was said or promised. A vague sense that "it's taking forever" is hard to act on; a dated record showing three missed callback promises is not.

Check what the insurer itself has promised

If an adjuster or representative gave you a specific date for a decision or callback, that missed date is a concrete, checkable fact — reference it directly in any follow-up rather than a general complaint about slowness.

Send one clear, written follow-up first

Before escalating, send a single written request for a status update and a specific response date. This creates the documented "reasonable attempt" that makes any later escalation look proportionate rather than premature.

When to escalate

Consider escalating when: a written follow-up goes unanswered past the date you gave, the insurer has missed its own previously stated timeline more than once, or you're getting inconsistent information from different representatives about the same claim.

Where to escalate

Start with the insurer's formal internal complaints process, if a routine follow-up hasn't worked. If that also stalls, your state's Department of Insurance handles unfair claims-handling practice complaints — most states have specific rules about how promptly insurers must acknowledge and process claims, and a documented pattern of delay is exactly what they're set up to review.
