No FENSA or Gas Safe Certificate? Why It Could Delay Selling Your Home

Replacing windows or a boiler without the right certification isn't just a paperwork gap — it's a genuine safety and legal issue that can stall a house sale years later.

What FENSA actually certifies

FENSA — the Fenestration Self-Assessment Scheme — was established in April 2002 to monitor Building Regulations compliance for replacement windows, doors, and roof lights in England and Wales. A FENSA certificate confirms the installer was a competent, registered member of the scheme and that the installation meets required thermal performance and safety standards. If you've replaced windows, doors, or roof lights since 1 April 2002, you need either a FENSA certificate (or the equivalent from Certass, another competent person scheme) or a separate Building Regulations compliance certificate.

Gas Safe: a legal requirement, not just good practice

Any boiler or gas appliance installation, repair, or servicing in the UK must be carried out by an engineer registered with Gas Safe Register. Using an unregistered installer for gas work isn't just a compliance gap — it's illegal, and creates a genuine risk of gas leaks, carbon monoxide poisoning, or fire from improperly installed or vented equipment.

Why this becomes a problem when you sell

Missing FENSA, Gas Safe, or equivalent documentation doesn't make your home unsellable, but it very often delays the process. Mortgage lenders frequently insist on all certificates being complete before lending against a property, and conveyancing solicitors typically ask for this documentation early. A missing certificate has become a genuine negotiating tool for buyers — some have been known to demand a price reduction to cover the cost of retrospective certification, even when the underlying work is perfectly sound.

Your two options if you're missing a certificate

  • Retrospective Building Regulation Compliance Certificate (regularisation): apply to your local authority's Building Control department, who will inspect the work and, if compliant, issue a retrospective compliance certificate. This typically costs between £300 and £400 and can take time, especially if remedial work is needed first.
  • Indemnity insurance: if the work was completed more than a year ago, indemnity insurance can cover potential costs if the local authority ever takes enforcement action — this is usually cheaper and faster than regularisation, but it doesn't confirm the work is actually compliant, only that you're financially protected if it isn't.

How to check if you already have valid certification

  1. Search your address on the FENSA website (a small fee applies) to check if a certificate is already on file for window/door work.
  2. Check the Gas Safe Register to confirm any gas engineer you've used was registered at the time of the work.
  3. If you can't find a certificate, contact the installer directly — they may still hold a copy even if you've misplaced yours.
  4. If genuinely no certificate exists, decide between regularisation and indemnity insurance based on your timeline and whether you're actively selling.

What this means practically

  • Any window, door, or roof light replacement since April 2002 needs a FENSA (or equivalent) certificate or a Building Regulations compliance certificate.
  • All gas work must be done by a Gas Safe registered engineer — this is a legal requirement with genuine safety implications, not just paperwork.
  • A missing certificate is fixable but not free — budget £300-£400 and some time for regularisation, or consider indemnity insurance for older work.
  • Sort this out well before you plan to sell — a missing certificate discovered mid-sale is a common and avoidable source of delay.
