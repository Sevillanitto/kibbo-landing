Ofsted's New Report Cards: What Training Providers Need to Know

The single-word verdicts of "Outstanding" or "Inadequate" no longer exist. Since November 2025, Ofsted issues a multi-area report card instead — a genuinely different system independent training providers need to prepare for differently.

The Single-Word Judgement Is Gone

If you're preparing for an Ofsted inspection based on older guidance, stop — the framework changed fundamentally. Single headline grades (Outstanding, Good, Requires Improvement, Inadequate) were removed with immediate effect for state schools in September 2024, and a full replacement framework using "report cards" took effect from 10 November 2025 across state-funded schools, with independent teacher education and non-association independent school inspections following from January 2026. Existing grades from before the change remain in place only until a provider's next inspection.

In place of one overall word, every inspected provider now receives a report card assessing performance across multiple evaluation areas — typically around the core areas of curriculum/education quality, behaviour and attitudes, personal development, and leadership and management, plus safeguarding — each scored on a new 5-point scale: urgent improvement, needs attention, expected standard, strong standard, and exceptional. Safeguarding specifically is judged separately on a binary "met" or "not met" basis, not on the 5-point scale.

Why This Changes How You Prepare

The old system meant one weak area could drag an otherwise strong provider down to a single damaging headline word. The new system is more granular — a provider can now show genuine strength in one area (e.g. teaching quality) while being flagged for improvement in another (e.g. leadership and governance), without one contaminating the other's public perception in the same binary way. Ofsted has stated providers aren't expected to create new evidence specifically for the new framework — the criteria are based on requirements and standards already in place — but the way you organize and present existing evidence for inspection should change to map against the new evaluation areas individually.

A provider that receives "urgent improvement" in any single area, or specific combinations of lower scores, can be placed in a formal "category of concern" — this is the modern equivalent of the old automatic funding-contract consequences tied to an "Inadequate" rating, so the practical stakes of a poor showing in any one area remain serious even though the terminology has changed.

Safeguarding and Prevent Duty Remain Non-Negotiable

Regardless of the framework change, safeguarding failures remain the fastest route to serious consequences — a "not met" safeguarding judgement is treated with particular severity given its binary, non-gradable nature. The statutory Prevent Duty (the requirement to have due regard to preventing people being drawn into terrorism) continues to apply to further education and training providers, and inspectors will expect to see a functioning, documented Prevent risk assessment and staff training record, not just a policy document that exists on paper.

Preparing Under the New Framework

  1. Review Ofsted's published inspection toolkit for your provider type — it sets out the specific criteria for each grade in each evaluation area, and is the actual reference document, not general guidance from before November 2025.
  2. Organize your existing evidence (curriculum documentation, quality assurance records, staff training logs) by evaluation area, since that's how inspectors will assess and report on it.
  3. Treat safeguarding and Prevent Duty compliance as a completely separate, non-negotiable track — a "not met" here can overshadow strong performance everywhere else.
  4. Prepare governors, trustees, or leadership to understand and explain the new 5-point scale and report card structure — communicating results to staff, learners, and funders under the new system is itself a task to plan for.
  5. Don't assume an existing pre-November-2025 grade reflects current reality — if your next inspection hasn't happened yet, prepare as though the new framework, not your old grade, is what matters.
