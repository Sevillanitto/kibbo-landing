How to Challenge and Remove a CIFAS Marker

A CIFAS marker doesn't arrive with a letter explaining itself — often the first sign is a bank account application that gets rejected for no clear reason.

What CIFAS actually is

CIFAS runs the UK's National Fraud Database, a shared system that banks, lenders, and other financial institutions use to flag accounts or applications associated with suspected fraud. A "CIFAS marker" placed against your name can cause other institutions to decline your applications for bank accounts, credit, insurance, or even certain jobs — often without ever telling you directly why.

How long a marker lasts

Marker duration depends on its type:

  • Victim of Impersonation marker: 13 months.
  • Protective Registration marker (one you can voluntarily apply for yourself after being a victim of identity fraud): 2 years.
  • First Party Fraud, Facility Takeover, or Misuse of Facility markers: up to 6 years.

Finding out if you have one

You have a free right under UK GDPR to submit a Data Subject Access Request (DSAR) directly to CIFAS to find out whether a marker exists against your name, and if so, which institution placed it and why. CIFAS must respond to a DSAR within one month.

The actual challenge process

  1. Contact the institution that placed the marker first. CIFAS itself doesn't investigate the underlying facts — the member institution that filed it does, and only that institution can remove or amend it.
  2. Ask for their evidence and explain your case, particularly if the marker resulted from identity theft rather than anything you actually did.
  3. If the institution won't remove it, ask CIFAS to review the case through its own internal dispute process.
  4. If you remain unsatisfied, escalate to the Financial Ombudsman Service, which can order a marker removed if it finds it was placed unfairly or without sufficient evidence.

What this means practically

  • Submit a free DSAR to CIFAS if you suspect a marker is blocking your applications — you have a right to know.
  • Direct your challenge at the institution that placed the marker, not CIFAS itself — that's who controls the actual data.
  • If you were a genuine victim of identity fraud, say so clearly and provide any police reference or evidence you have.
  • Escalate to the Financial Ombudsman Service if the institution and CIFAS's internal review both fail to resolve it fairly.

Sources

  • CIFAS — submitting a Data Subject Access Request. cifas.org.uk/dsar
  • Financial Ombudsman Service — complaints about fraud markers and CIFAS data. financial-ombudsman.org.uk
