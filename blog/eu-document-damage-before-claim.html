How to Document Damage Before Making an Insurance Claim

Whatever was damaged — a home, a car, or personal belongings — the documentation logic is the same, and doing it before you file changes how smoothly the claim goes.

Photograph before you clean up or repair

Wide shots for context, close-ups for detail, taken as soon as it's safe to do so and before any temporary or permanent repairs begin. Photos taken after clean-up are far less convincing than ones showing the original state of the damage.

Gather invoices and proof of value

Original purchase invoices are strongest; bank or card statements are a reasonable substitute where they're unavailable. For items without a clear price record, an independent valuation supports a stronger claim than a self-estimated figure.

Establish ownership

Warranty documents, serial numbers, or photos showing the item in regular use all help confirm ownership, particularly for items without a surviving receipt.

Get repair or replacement estimates

Quotes from more than one source, where practical, support a fair valuation and give you a stronger position if the insurer's own assessment comes in lower than expected.

Build a clear timeline

Note the exact date and circumstances of the incident, and any immediate actions taken. A precise timeline makes it easier to demonstrate that damage occurred when and how you say it did, rather than leaving room for the insurer to question the sequence of events.
