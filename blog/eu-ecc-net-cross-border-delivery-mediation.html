ECC-Net: Free Cross-Border Mediation for a Delivery Dispute With an EU Seller

You don't need a lawyer in another country to fight a delivery dispute with a seller based there. The European Consumer Centres Network mediates for free — just note the old ODR platform this used to work alongside is no longer active.

What ECC-Net Actually Does

When a seller based in another EU member state won't resolve a delivery or refund dispute directly, the European Consumer Centres Network (ECC-Net) — funded jointly by the European Commission and member states — provides free mediation without requiring you to hire a lawyer or navigate a foreign country's courts yourself.

The process typically runs: you first attempt to resolve the issue directly with the seller in writing. If that fails, your own country's ECC office reviews your case — invoices, correspondence, tracking numbers — and if it has legal merit, forwards your file to the ECC office in the seller's country, which then contacts the trader directly to press for a resolution.

Important Correction: The EU ODR Platform Is No Longer Active

The EU's Online Dispute Resolution (ODR) platform, which for years operated as a companion tool alongside ECC-Net for online purchase disputes, was discontinued in July 2025. If you've seen older guidance referencing the ODR platform as a place to submit a complaint, that information is now outdated — it's no longer a functioning route. ECC-Net itself remains fully active, along with national ADR body directories, which now serve as the current primary channels for this kind of dispute.

If Mediation Doesn't Resolve It

If a trader refuses to engage even with ECC-Net's mediation, two further routes exist:

  • National ADR bodies: Directive 2013/11/EU guarantees consumers access to independent alternative dispute resolution entities covering e-commerce and delivery-related disputes — check your country's official ADR body directory for the one relevant to your sector.
  • European Small Claims Procedure: for cross-border disputes up to €5,000 (the threshold was raised from €2,000 under Regulation (EU) 2015/2421), you can pursue a simplified court procedure using standardized forms, without needing to hire local legal representation in the seller's country.

How to Get Started

  1. Attempt to resolve the dispute directly with the seller first, in writing, and keep a copy of everything.
  2. Contact your own country's ECC office (not the seller's) as your first point of contact — they handle the cross-border referral on your behalf.
  3. Have your invoice, order confirmation, tracking information, and all correspondence with the seller ready — this is what determines whether your case has a viable legal basis to pursue.
  4. If ECC-Net mediation doesn't resolve it, ask about the relevant national ADR body for your specific type of dispute before considering the Small Claims Procedure.
