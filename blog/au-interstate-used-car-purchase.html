Buying a Used Car Across Australian States: Registration, Inspections and Hidden Costs

Interstate listings can look like a bargain until you add up everything getting the car home and re-registered actually involves.

Re-registration isn't automatic

Bringing a car into a new state or territory generally requires re-registering it there, which can include a vehicle identity/safety check and a compliance plate or inspection specific to that jurisdiction's rules — not just a paperwork transfer.

Transfer fees and stamp duty

Stamp duty (vehicle transfer duty) is calculated differently by state and is generally payable in the state where you register the car, not where you bought it — so the sale price alone doesn't tell you the real cost until you know your home state's rate.

Inspections you may need before registration

Depending on the state, you may need a roadworthy/safety certificate valid in your home jurisdiction (an interstate seller's certificate may not be accepted), and possibly a separate identity check confirming the VIN and engine number match the paperwork.

Transport and insurance

Factor in the cost of getting the car from the seller's state to yours — whether you drive it (and what interim/transit registration or insurance that requires) or use a transport service — plus confirming your insurer will cover the vehicle from the point of purchase, not just from re-registration.

Do the total-cost math before you commit

Add the purchase price, stamp duty in your home state, any required inspections, transfer fees, and transport, then compare that total to a similar car listed locally — the interstate "bargain" sometimes isn't one once every cost is counted.
