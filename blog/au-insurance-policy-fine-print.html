The Insurance Policy Fine Print: Exclusions, Conditions and Limits Explained

The Product Disclosure Statement is long for a reason — most of what determines a claim's outcome lives in the sections people skim past.

Read the Product Disclosure Statement, not just the summary

The Product Disclosure Statement (PDS) is the actual policy document, and it's what governs a claim — not the shorter marketing summary or comparison table you may have used when buying. Any specific concern deserves a direct check against the PDS itself.

Exclusions: entire categories removed

Exclusions remove specific causes of loss from coverage entirely, regardless of the sum insured. Common examples include gradual deterioration or wear, and certain natural peril types depending on the insurer and policy — always check the specific exclusions list rather than assuming based on the policy's general name.

Conditions: requirements you must meet

Conditions are things you must do or maintain for coverage to apply — security requirements, maintenance obligations, or notification deadlines after an incident. A missed condition can affect a claim just as much as an exclusion, so check these sections with equal care.

Limits and sub-limits: the ceiling, even when something is covered

A policy's overall sum insured doesn't guarantee a specific category or item is covered up to that same figure — sub-limits for things like jewellery, cash, or specific types of damage are common and often lower than people expect.

Questions worth asking before assuming coverage

Ask the insurer directly, in writing, whether a specific concern is covered rather than relying on the policy's general description. Ask what conditions apply to that specific type of cover, and whether any sub-limit applies to the category of item or damage you're asking about.
