Responsible Service of Alcohol: Protecting Your Liquor Licence in Australia

Serving alcohol to an unduly intoxicated patron carries personal fines for the staff member and the licensee — and in Queensland, two convictions in two years triggers automatic licence suspension.

Penalties Apply to Staff, Not Just the Business

Responsible Service of Alcohol (RSA) obligations exist in every Australian state and territory, and breaches carry penalties that fall on the individual staff member serving the drink, separately from penalties on the licensee. In NSW, for example, an employee who serves an unduly intoxicated patron can personally face fines reaching into the thousands of dollars, while the licensee faces a substantially higher penalty on top of that — and repeat or serious breaches can trigger director liability as well. Penalty amounts and structures vary meaningfully by state and are typically indexed or revised annually, so always check your specific state's current liquor licensing authority for the exact figures rather than relying on a figure from another jurisdiction or an older source.

What "Unduly Intoxicated" Actually Means in Practice

RSA law doesn't require staff to make a precise medical or scientific determination of blood alcohol level — the standard is behavioral: visible signs like slurred speech, loss of coordination, aggressive or disorderly behavior, or an obvious inability to stand or walk steadily are the practical indicators inspectors and courts look for. A patron doesn't need to be visibly falling-down drunk for staff to have a legal obligation to refuse further service — the threshold is lower than many venues assume, and erring toward refusal is the legally safer position.

Structural Consequences Beyond the Fine

Some states impose consequences that go beyond a monetary penalty. In Queensland, for example, a licensee convicted of two offenses relating to minors or intoxicated persons within a two-year period faces automatic licence suspension — this isn't discretionary once the threshold is met. Compliance history, including infringement notices and prosecutions short of conviction, can also affect a venue's annual licensing fee through risk-based assessment, meaning the cost of a breach extends well past the immediate fine.

Building a Defensible RSA Culture

  1. Confirm every staff member serving alcohol holds current, valid RSA certification — recertification cycles have shortened in some states, so track expiry dates actively rather than assuming certificates remain valid indefinitely.
  2. Give staff explicit permission and backing to refuse service — the awkwardness of refusing a known or regular customer is where RSA breaches most often happen, not from ignorance of the rules.
  3. Document incidents where service was refused or a patron was removed — this record protects the venue if a dispute or complaint follows.
  4. Train security and floor staff on early intervention (slowing service, offering water, checking in) rather than waiting until a patron is obviously intoxicated to act.
  5. Review your state's specific current penalty amounts and licence suspension triggers at least annually, since these figures and thresholds are revised over time.
