How to Report an Unsafe Product in the EU: Using the Safety Gate System

If you buy something online that turns out to be genuinely dangerous — a charger that melts, a children's toy with parts that detach — it's easy to assume your only option is asking the seller for a refund, and that European authorities don't get involved in individual purchases. That's not accurate. The EU runs Safety Gate, one of the most advanced rapid-alert systems in the world for dangerous non-food products, and any consumer can report directly into it.

What Safety Gate actually is

Under the General Product Safety Regulation (GPSR), any consumer can report a serious safety fault directly. Your individual report feeds a surveillance network that can force an immediate product withdrawal across all 27 member states and result in serious penalties for the manufacturer — this isn't just a national-level complaint, it's genuinely pan-European in reach.

The scale of the system

Safety Gate processes a substantial volume of alerts every year — recent annual reports have shown figures in the thousands. Cosmetics, toys, and electrical appliances are consistently among the most frequently flagged product categories, with chemical risks and injury/choking hazards among the most common risk types identified. Exact year-to-year figures shift, so check the Commission's latest published Safety Gate report if you want current statistics rather than relying on a fixed number.

Filing an effective report

Access the official Safety Gate consumer portal (Safety Gate: Report). Describe the incident in detail, and attach clear photographs of the brand, model, batch number, and barcode. Specify exactly where you purchased the item.

What happens after you submit

Your report is passed to your national consumer authority for technical assessment. Under the GPSR's newer rules, online marketplaces are required to register with the system and act quickly — removing sales listings within a short, legally mandated window (commonly cited as around two business days) once authorities confirm a genuine risk.

What this doesn't replace

Reporting to Safety Gate is about public safety, not personal compensation — it doesn't itself get you a refund for the specific item you bought. If you also want your money back or a replacement, that's a separate consumer rights claim against the seller under the standard legal guarantee rules.
