Internet Outages in the US: When Can You Demand a Billing Credit?

Your internet was down for days, but the provider refuses a credit. There is no single federal rule requiring a prorated refund for every broadband outage.

There is no universal federal prorated-refund rule

The FCC accepts broadband billing and service complaints, but does not impose a blanket rule requiring every provider to prorate every outage automatically. Provider agreements and state rules can create additional rights.

Start with the service agreement

Check outage, billing and service-credit terms, including minimum outage durations and request procedures. Keep the full agreement, including arbitration provisions.

Document the outage

  • Start/end time.
  • Provider outage notices.
  • Router or modem records where available.
  • Support tickets and case numbers.
  • Invoices showing the billed period.

Ask for a specific credit

Calculate the amount you believe should be credited based on the provider's policy or contract and explain the non-service period.

Escalate

The FCC may serve telecom billing/service complaints on the provider, which generally has up to 30 days to respond. State regulators can also be relevant depending on the service.

What this means practically

  • Do not promise an automatic federal credit.
  • Use the provider's actual credit policy.
  • Document the outage and every support interaction.
  • Escalate through the appropriate federal or state route.
