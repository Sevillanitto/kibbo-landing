The PPSR Check: Why Australian Used-Car Buyers Shouldn't Skip It

It's the single cheapest, fastest check you can run before buying a used car in Australia — and one of the most skipped.

What a PPSR search actually reveals

Run at the official government site, ppsr.gov.au, a search by VIN or registration number shows whether the vehicle has a registered security interest (money owed on it under a loan or lease), whether it's recorded as stolen, and whether it carries a written-off marker.

Why this matters more than buyers assume

If a car is sold with an undischarged finance interest still registered against it, the financier can, in some circumstances, repossess the vehicle from you — the new owner — even though you bought it in good faith and paid the seller in full. A PPSR search before paying is what prevents this.

Use the official site only

Third-party sites often charge more than the official government fee for the same underlying data, and some misleadingly present themselves as the official register. Go directly to ppsr.gov.au to avoid paying extra for a search you can run yourself for a small, fixed fee.

What a PPSR search doesn't cover

It won't tell you about the vehicle's mechanical condition, non-statutory (repairable) write-off history in every state, or genuine odometer accuracy — pair it with a service history check and, ideally, an independent inspection.
