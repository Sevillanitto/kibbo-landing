Habitability Assessment: How to Document Pests, Mold, and Unsanitary Conditions Against a Landlord's Denial

"It's not that bad" is the most common landlord response to a habitability complaint — and the way you counter it isn't with more urgency, it's with more measurement.

Photos Alone Are Often Not Enough

A photo of mold or a pest sighting proves the problem existed at one moment — it doesn't establish severity, cause, or an ongoing pattern, all of which matter if a landlord disputes your claim or blames tenant behavior. A stronger habitability file combines visual evidence with objective measurement and, where the issue is serious enough, third-party verification.

Building an Evidence File That Holds Up

  • Humidity readings. An inexpensive hygrometer lets you log the actual humidity level in an affected room over time, not just describe it as "damp." Consistent readings above normal indoor humidity ranges, logged with dates, help establish that a mold problem is structural (a leak, poor ventilation, rising damp) rather than caused by tenant behavior — which matters, since many jurisdictions place responsibility for structural moisture issues on the landlord, but responsibility for tenant-caused condensation (not ventilating, drying laundry indoors) on the tenant.
  • Professional pest inspections. A licensed pest control inspection report is far more persuasive evidence than photos of droppings or a single sighting — it establishes the type of infestation, its likely source, and often an assessment of how long it's been present, which can help counter a landlord's claim that it's a new or tenant-caused problem.
  • Medical documentation, where relevant. If a respiratory condition or other health issue has developed or worsened and a doctor connects it, even tentatively, to the property's condition, that medical record becomes part of your evidence file. This is sensitive information — only include it if genuinely relevant, and be selective about who you share it with outside a formal dispute process.
  • A dated, continuous photo log, not a single before/after pair — showing progression over time is often more persuasive than a single snapshot, since it counters the argument that the problem is new or minor.

Notify in Writing Before You Do Anything Else

Before commissioning any inspection or spending money on documentation tools, send the landlord written notice of the issue first. In most jurisdictions, the landlord needs a reasonable opportunity to respond before you can rely on self-arranged remedies or escalate — jumping straight to a paid inspection without first giving written notice can weaken your position, even if the underlying problem is completely genuine.

What This Means Practically

  • Always notify the landlord in writing first, before commissioning any paid inspection or spending money on documentation.
  • Use objective tools (a hygrometer, a professional pest report) alongside photos, not instead of them.
  • Keep a continuous, dated log rather than a single before/after photo pair — it demonstrates pattern and severity more convincingly.
  • Handle any medical documentation carefully and only include it where it's genuinely relevant to the dispute.
