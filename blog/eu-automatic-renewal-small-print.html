Can an EU Company Hide Automatic Renewal in the Small Print?

You didn't actively renew. The company says the contract renewed automatically because it was in the terms. The question is whether the renewal clause was clear, transparent and fair — not simply whether it existed somewhere.

A Term Can Exist and Still Be Unfair

EU unfair-contract rules do not say that automatic renewal is always prohibited. They do, however, require standard terms to be drafted in plain, intelligible language and protect consumers against terms that create a significant imbalance contrary to good faith.

Your Europe specifically identifies two relevant categories: hidden terms that consumers could not easily have been aware of before signing, and automatic extensions where the consumer must give notice by an unreasonably early deadline.

Where Should You Look for the Renewal Clause?

Check the subscription checkout, order confirmation and terms that were supplied at the time of contracting. Look for wording such as "renews automatically", "continues until cancelled", "notice required before renewal", or a specific renewal date.

Do not rely only on the trader's current terms page. Keep the version that applied when you subscribed.

Small Print Is Not a Magic Shield

A trader cannot make an unclear or unfair term automatically enforceable just by placing it in a long document labelled "terms and conditions". EU rules require transparency, and ambiguous wording is interpreted in favour of consumers under the Unfair Contract Terms Directive.

That does not mean every clause near the bottom of a page is legally "hidden". The assessment depends on the circumstances, presentation and national law.

Automatic Renewal Deserves Special Attention

Your Europe lists automatic extensions of fixed-duration contracts among potentially unfair terms when the consumer must provide notice by an unreasonably early deadline. The site's example describes a one-year mobile contract automatically extended for another year because the consumer had failed to give six months' notice.

This is a useful guide, not a universal rule that every renewal clause with a particular number of months is unlawful.

What Should You Document?

  1. The exact renewal clause.
  2. The date you joined.
  3. The date the initial term ended.
  4. The notice deadline.
  5. Any reminder or renewal message you received.
  6. The charge taken after renewal.
  7. Your cancellation request and the trader's response.

If the Company Relies on the Clause

Ask the trader to identify the exact wording and provide the terms applicable when you subscribed. Then assess whether the clause was transparent and whether its operation created an unfair imbalance.

For cross-border disputes, the European Consumer Centres Network can assist consumers with problems involving traders in another EU country. National consumer authorities and national consumer organisations also play a role in enforcement and advice.

What This Means for You

  • Automatic renewal is not automatically unlawful across the EU.
  • A hidden or opaque renewal term can raise an unfair-terms issue.
  • Record the notice deadline, not just the renewal date.
  • Keep the version of the terms that applied when you subscribed.
  • Use the national and cross-border consumer routes available for your specific case.
