Fake AI Trading Bots and Cloud Mining Scams: How to Identify and Report Them to ASIC and ReportCyber

The dashboard shows your balance growing every day. That's the point — it's designed to keep you depositing, right up until the site disappears.

The Pattern Behind "AI Trading" and "Cloud Mining" Schemes

These schemes present themselves as sophisticated technology — an AI trading algorithm, or a cloud mining operation you can invest in without owning hardware — but functionally, they're classic Ponzi structures: early depositors are paid "returns" using money from later depositors, not from any actual trading or mining activity. The platform typically shows a dashboard with a steadily growing balance, reinforcing trust and encouraging larger deposits, right up until the operators disappear with whatever remains.

Common markers: guaranteed or unusually consistent daily/weekly returns regardless of market conditions, a website that's recently registered despite claims of years of operation, and increasing pressure to deposit more to "unlock" higher tiers or withdraw existing funds.

Where to Report in Australia

  • ReportCyber (Australian Cyber Security Centre): the national portal for reporting cybercrime, including online investment fraud — file here first, with as much detail as you have.
  • ASIC: worth notifying separately if the scheme involved marketing an investment product or made specific claims about registered financial services — ASIC's role is distinct from the police-facing cybercrime report.
  • Scamwatch (ACCC): for the consumer-fraud reporting and pattern-tracking angle specifically.

Documenting the Financial Trail

Before or immediately after reporting, document:

  • Every wallet address you sent funds to, and the transaction hashes for each transfer.
  • Screenshots of the platform's dashboard, marketing claims, and any communication with "support" or "account managers."
  • Whether funds moved to a wallet that later touched a known centralized exchange — this is the detail that gives investigators a realistic chance of tracing further, since exchanges perform identity verification that anonymous wallets don't.

What This Means for You

Report immediately rather than waiting to see if the platform resolves the issue — these schemes typically vanish quickly once withdrawal requests spike, and early reports contribute to broader investigations even when individual recovery isn't likely. Treat any "guaranteed" or unusually consistent return claim as a red flag regardless of how sophisticated the platform's branding looks.
