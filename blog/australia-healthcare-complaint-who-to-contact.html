Something Went Wrong With Your Healthcare in Australia: Who Do You Actually Complain To?

The doctor says it's the hospital's problem. The hospital says it's the doctor's. And your complaint goes nowhere — because Australia genuinely splits this responsibility differently depending on where you live.

Why This Is Genuinely More Complicated in Australia Than Most Countries

Most Australian health practitioners are regulated nationally by the Australian Health Practitioner Regulation Agency (AHPRA) — but two states run their own separate system entirely. In New South Wales, the Health Care Complaints Commission (HCCC) — an independent statutory body established in 1994 under the Health Care Complaints Act 1993 — handles complaints about health practitioners and services, not AHPRA. In Queensland, the Office of the Health Ombudsman plays the equivalent role. Everywhere else, AHPRA and the relevant National Board manage notifications about individual practitioners directly.

Match the Type of Complaint to the Right Body

  • Concern about a specific practitioner's conduct, health, or performance (a registered doctor, nurse, dentist, etc.): AHPRA nationally — except in NSW (HCCC) and Queensland (Office of the Health Ombudsman).
  • Concern about how AHPRA, a National Board, or an accreditation authority itself handled your matter or your personal information: the National Health Practitioner Ombudsman and Privacy Commissioner — a genuinely separate body from AHPRA, specifically overseeing AHPRA's own conduct.
  • Privacy complaint about health information generally: this depends on your state. Victoria has its own Health Complaints Commissioner administering the Health Records Act 2001. NSW has the NSW Information and Privacy Commission. The ACT Health Services Commissioner handles ACT health record privacy complaints specifically, separate from the general ACT Privacy Commissioner. For federally-regulated providers not covered by a state scheme, the OAIC is the right body.
  • Concern about a hospital, health service, or general care experience rather than an individual practitioner: most states have a separate general health complaints body (in Western Australia, for example, the Health and Disability Services Complaints Office, distinct from AHPRA entirely).

Why Identifying the Right Body First Saves Real Time

Complaints bodies in Australia generally expect you to have raised the concern with the practitioner, practice, or organisation directly first, where possible. But sending your complaint to the wrong statutory body from the start — AHPRA when you're in NSW, for instance — doesn't just waste time; these organisations will typically redirect you rather than act, meaning you effectively start the clock over.

What This Means Practically

  • Identify your state or territory first, and check whether it has its own practitioner complaints body (NSW, Queensland) before defaulting to AHPRA.
  • Separate the type of complaint clearly: is it about a specific practitioner's conduct, a privacy/data handling issue, or a broader service/hospital experience — each may go to a different body.
  • Raise the concern directly with the provider first where it's safe and reasonable to do so, before escalating to a statutory body.
  • If you're genuinely unsure which body applies, AHPRA's own website maintains a list of state-based health complaints organisations and can point you toward the right one.
