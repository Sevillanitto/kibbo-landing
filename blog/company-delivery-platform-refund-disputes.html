Disputing Unfair Refunds and Withheld Payments From Uber Eats and Deliveroo

A customer claims "food was cold" or "never arrived," an algorithm approves the refund instantly, and the deduction comes straight out of the restaurant's next payout — often with no meaningful path to appeal. Here's how to actually push back.

Why Automated Refund Decisions Favor the Customer by Default

Delivery platforms process an enormous volume of "item missing," "order never arrived," or "food arrived cold" complaints, and the economics favor fast, automated resolution over investigation: a small refund settled instantly protects the platform's customer satisfaction metrics far more cheaply than a manual review of every claim. The practical effect for restaurants is that many refunds are approved with no human review of what actually happened in the kitchen — the deduction is simply applied against the restaurant's next payout statement, sometimes bundled anonymously among dozens of other line items.

Where the Merchant Terms Actually Give You Leverage

Every delivery platform's Merchant Terms (the contract a restaurant agrees to when joining the platform) sets out — usually deep in the document — what evidence a restaurant is entitled to dispute a refund with, and what response time the platform commits to. This is the leverage point most restaurants never read closely enough to use: most platforms provide a formal merchant dispute or support-ticket process specifically for refund deductions, separate from general customer support, and disputes submitted through the correct channel with the specific evidence the terms describe get materially better outcomes than a generic complaint email.

Building an Evidence-Backed Dispute

  • Pickup timestamps — most platforms log the exact time an order was marked ready and the exact time the courier collected it; if the gap is short and normal, this directly rebuts a "cold food" claim caused by kitchen delay.
  • Kitchen video footage, where available, showing the order being packed and handed off within the platform's own stated preparation window.
  • Packaging photos taken at handoff, particularly for orders using insulated bags or thermal packaging that would contradict a "cold on arrival" claim if the courier's delivery time was excessive.
  • The platform's own delivery-time data for that order, which in many disputes shows the courier took significantly longer than the platform's average — shifting the cause of "cold food" to delivery time, not kitchen performance.

Escalating a Dispute Properly

  1. Submit the dispute through the platform's dedicated merchant support channel, not general customer service, and reference the specific order ID and the specific Merchant Terms clause covering refund disputes.
  2. Attach evidence in the format the platform requests — many merchant portals have a specific upload flow for dispute evidence that a generic email bypasses entirely.
  3. Request a specific response timeline in writing, and follow up in writing exactly when that deadline passes without a response.
  4. Track every dispute — amount, date, evidence submitted, outcome — since a documented pattern of frequent, unresolved deductions is itself useful evidence if you need to escalate to a regulator or industry body.
  5. If disputes are repeatedly rejected without genuine review, consider raising the pattern with your national competition or consumer protection authority — platforms' refund practices have drawn regulatory scrutiny in multiple jurisdictions.
