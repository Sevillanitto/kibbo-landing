How to Complain to the Telecommunications Industry Ombudsman (TIO)

Telstra, Optus, TPG and every other Australian phone and internet provider must belong to the TIO scheme by law. What most people don't realise is that the fee structure gives providers a real financial reason to fix your problem before it escalates.

Why membership isn't optional for providers

Under the Telecommunications (Consumer Protection and Service Standards) Act 1999, carriers and eligible carriage service providers are legally required to join the TIO scheme. It's a free, independent dispute resolution service for individual consumers, small businesses and not-for-profits who can't resolve a complaint directly with their phone or internet provider.

Try your provider first

The TIO expects you to give your provider a genuine chance to resolve the issue before stepping in. If they haven't resolved it within a reasonable time, or you're not satisfied with their response, that's when the TIO can help.

Why providers have a real incentive to settle early

The TIO recovers its running costs from member fees, and those fees are structured so that complaints resolved quickly and simply cost providers much less than complaints that escalate to conciliation or a full investigation. This isn't a minor administrative detail — it's a deliberate design that gives providers a genuine financial reason to resolve your complaint fairly and fast, rather than let it climb the escalation ladder.

How the process works

  1. Raise the issue with your provider directly first and keep a record of what was discussed and promised.
  2. If unresolved, lodge a complaint with the TIO online, referencing your provider's response (or lack of one).
  3. You'll receive a TIO reference number — keep this for all further communication.
  4. The TIO may refer the complaint back to the provider for a fast resolution, or move to conciliation or a full investigation if needed.
  5. For faulty service disputes, either you or the provider can arrange an independent technician if you disagree about the cause of a fault.

What the TIO can actually help with

  • Billing disputes, including cancellation charges, unclear bills, and overcharging
  • Faulty services and equipment, assessed against Australian Consumer Law consumer guarantees
  • Financial hardship situations affecting your ability to keep up with a payment plan
  • Property or land access disputes involving telecom infrastructure
  • Service disruptions from natural disasters

What this means practically

  • The TIO is free for consumers — there's no cost to you at any stage of the process.
  • Always contact your provider first; the TIO expects this and will usually check.
  • Providers face real, escalating costs the longer a dispute drags on, which is genuine leverage in your favour once a case is lodged.
  • Keep every reference number and written record — they matter if your case moves to conciliation or investigation.
