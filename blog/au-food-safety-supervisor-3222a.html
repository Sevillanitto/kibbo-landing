Food Safety Supervisor Requirements Under Standard 3.2.2A

Since December 8, 2023, the requirement for a certified Food Safety Supervisor is standardized nationwide — including states like WA, SA, NT, and Tasmania that had no equivalent requirement before.

What Changed on December 8, 2023

Standard 3.2.2A — Food Safety Management Tools, part of the Australia New Zealand Food Standards Code, introduced three mandatory tools for eligible food service and retail businesses across all Australian states and territories: a certified Food Safety Supervisor (FSS), food handler training, and evidence records substantiating that critical food safety controls are actually being managed. Before this standard, FSS requirements existed only in some states; Western Australia, South Australia, the Northern Territory, and Tasmania had no equivalent requirement until Standard 3.2.2A brought national consistency.

Category One vs. Category Two Businesses

Which tools apply depends on your business category. Category one businesses — those that process and handle unpackaged, potentially hazardous, ready-to-eat food on-site, such as most restaurants, bakeries, and caterers — must implement all three tools. Category two businesses — those that retail potentially hazardous, ready-to-eat food that was handled or packed on-site but not made or processed there, such as delis or service stations — need a Food Safety Supervisor and trained food handlers, but not the full substantiation/evidence tool. Class 3 and class 4 food businesses are exempt from Standard 3.2.2A itself, though they must still ensure food handlers have relevant food safety skills.

The FSS Certification Requirement

A Food Safety Supervisor must hold recognized, formal certification obtained within the past five years, typically covering competency units such as SITXFSA005 (Use hygienic practices for food safety) and SITXFSA006 (Participate in safe food handling practices), or SIRRFSA001 for retail settings, issued by a Registered Training Organisation. The FSS must be "reasonably available" to advise and supervise food handlers during food handling operations — not necessarily on-site every minute, but genuinely accessible when needed. When a certificate expires, most jurisdictions (NSW among them) allow roughly 30 operational days to recertify or appoint a replacement FSS before the business is out of compliance.

Food Handler Training and Evidence Records

Every food handler engaged in a prescribed activity must complete a recognized food safety training course, or otherwise demonstrate appropriate skills and knowledge — this applies even to staff who aren't the designated FSS. For category one businesses, evidence records must substantiate that critical controls (safe receiving, storage, processing, display, and transport of potentially hazardous food, plus cleaning and sanitising) are genuinely being met — records can include completed forms, digital logs, photos, or a documented standard operating procedure staff can demonstrate to an inspector on request.

Getting Compliant

  1. Confirm your business's category (one or two) using FSANZ's decision tree, since this determines which tools you must implement.
  2. Check every current FSS certificate's issue date — certification is only valid for five years, and recertification windows are shorter than most operators expect once expired.
  3. Confirm food handler training is documented for every staff member handling high-risk food, not just for management.
  4. Set up a simple, consistent evidence recordkeeping system now rather than reconstructing records after an inspection notice.
  5. Check whether your state has layered additional requirements on top of the national standard (NSW's Food Regulation 2025, for example, added a single-RTO training rule).
