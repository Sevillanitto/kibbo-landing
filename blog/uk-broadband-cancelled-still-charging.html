You Cancelled Your Broadband. Why Is the Provider Still Charging You?

Your internet service ended. The Direct Debit didn't. The first step is to separate a valid final bill from a payment that should no longer have been collected.

A Payment After Cancellation Is Not Automatically a Billing Error

A broadband contract can have a notice period, a final bill, unpaid usage, an early termination charge, or other contractual amounts that remain payable after service stops. The key question is whether the amount collected matches the provider's contract and Ofcom's applicable rules.

For consumer fixed broadband and telephone services, Ofcom says the required cancellation notice should never be more than one month. A different situation can arise when a contract is ended through a switch under Ofcom's switching arrangements.

Start by Checking the Cancellation Date

  1. When did you submit the cancellation or switch request?
  2. What date did the provider confirm as the termination date?
  3. Did the provider say a notice period applied?
  4. When did the service actually stop?
  5. What dates does the final bill cover?

Keep the cancellation confirmation, the final bill and the original contract together. The dates often explain whether the last payment was expected.

What About Equipment Charges?

Routers and other equipment can have return or non-return consequences under the provider's terms. Do not assume a charge is unlawful simply because the broadband service has ended.

Check what equipment the contract says must be returned, the return deadline, and whether the provider has confirmed receipt. Keep proof of postage or collection.

The Direct Debit Is a Payment Instruction — Not the Contract Itself

MoneyHelper and the Direct Debit scheme explain that you can cancel a Direct Debit through your bank, but stopping the payment does not by itself cancel the underlying contract or any genuine amount you still owe. If money is taken after a Direct Debit has been cancelled, you can contact your bank and claim a refund under the Direct Debit Guarantee.

That means the safest sequence is usually: cancel the broadband service properly, obtain the final bill, dispute any incorrect amounts, and monitor the Direct Debit separately.

If the Provider Keeps Charging You

Citizens Advice advises consumers who receive a bill after cancelling or switching to check that they followed the provider's cancellation process, ask the company for a refund and to stop taking payments, and make a formal complaint if the problem continues.

If the provider does not resolve the complaint, you may be able to use the ADR scheme the provider belongs to. Ofcom requires telecoms providers to belong to an approved ADR scheme.

What This Means for You

  • Save the exact cancellation confirmation and termination date.
  • Check whether the final payment relates to a valid notice period or final bill.
  • Keep evidence of equipment return.
  • Do not confuse stopping a Direct Debit with cancelling the broadband contract.
  • If the provider keeps collecting money after cancellation, dispute it and use the formal complaints process.
  • Use ADR if the complaint remains unresolved and the scheme is available for your provider.
