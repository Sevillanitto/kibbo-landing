RPL Fast-Track Qualifications: How to Spot a Fraudulent Provider

A legitimate Recognition of Prior Learning assessment involves real scrutiny of your actual work evidence. A provider promising a qualification in days, with minimal evidence required, is a pattern ASQA has repeatedly cancelled certifications over.

What RPL Is Actually Supposed to Involve

Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL) is a legitimate assessment approach that allows a person with existing skills and experience — gained through work, informal learning, or overseas qualifications — to have that competency formally assessed against the requirements of an Australian nationally recognized qualification, without having to repeat training they've already effectively mastered. Done properly, it's a genuinely valuable pathway, particularly for experienced tradespeople and skilled workers who never had access to formal training pathways.

A rigorous RPL assessment requires a trained assessor to evaluate real evidence against the specific units of competency in the qualification — this typically includes things like third-party reports from supervisors or employers, work samples, photos or video of the candidate performing relevant tasks, payslips or employment records establishing relevant experience, and often a practical demonstration or structured interview.

The Fraud Pattern

Fraudulent or low-quality RPL providers sell a qualification based on minimal, superficial evidence review — sometimes little more than a short questionnaire and a fee — issuing certificates that don't reflect a genuine competency assessment. When ASQA identifies this pattern during an audit or investigation, the consequence isn't limited to a warning: it can result in the qualifications themselves being cancelled, which means holders can be left with a certificate that's no longer valid, despite having paid for it and possibly relied on it for employment or licensing purposes.

Red Flags to Watch For

  • A provider that promises a qualification in days, with no substantive evidence-gathering process described.
  • An assessment process that consists mainly or entirely of a written questionnaire, with no request for verifiable third-party evidence (supervisor statements, payslips, work samples).
  • No practical demonstration, interview, or direct assessor engagement at any point.
  • Marketing that emphasizes speed and low cost over the specific units of competency being assessed.
  • Reluctance to explain, when asked directly, exactly what evidence will be required and how it maps to the qualification's actual requirements.

Protecting Yourself

  1. Confirm the provider is a currently registered RTO with the specific qualification on their scope of registration — check this directly against the national training register, not just the provider's own marketing claims.
  2. Ask specifically what evidence will be required before you pay anything, and compare it against the qualification's actual units of competency, publicly available on the national training register.
  3. Be skeptical of any RPL process that doesn't involve some form of direct assessor interaction — a practical demonstration, structured interview, or genuine evidence review takes real time.
  4. Build a genuine, well-organized portfolio of your actual evidence (reference letters, payslips, photos/video of work performed, prior overseas or informal qualifications) — this is what protects both a legitimate assessment and your own interests if a provider's practices are later questioned.
  5. If you already hold an RPL-based qualification and are concerned about the provider's legitimacy, you can check the qualification's current validity status through the training register.
