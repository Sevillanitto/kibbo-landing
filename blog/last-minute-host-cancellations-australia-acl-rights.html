Double Booking and Last-Minute Cancellation in Vacation Rentals: Relocation and Refund Rights Under the ACL

When a host cancels your peak-season booking at the last minute, the law guarantees you a refund or remedy — the "we'll rebook you somewhere better" promise is the platform's own policy, not a legal mandate.

What the ACL Actually Guarantees, and What It Doesn't

Under the Australian Consumer Law (ACL), consumer guarantees — including that a service will be provided with due care and skill, and that it will be reasonably fit for the purpose disclosed — apply to bookings made through platforms like Airbnb and Stayz, and these guarantees generally can't be excluded or limited by a platform's or host's terms and conditions. If a host cancels a confirmed booking, or the accommodation isn't provided as described, the guest is entitled to a refund or another appropriate remedy under the ACL, regardless of what a restrictive cancellation policy might otherwise say.

What the ACL does not specifically mandate is that a platform must fund relocation to an equivalent or superior property at no additional cost. That specific promise — rebooking assistance at comparable pricing, depending on availability — is Airbnb's own Rebooking and Refund Policy, a contractual commitment the platform has chosen to make, not a baseline legal requirement imposed by the ACL. It's a genuinely useful protection, but it's important not to overstate its legal source: it's platform policy operating on top of your ACL rights, not a description of what the ACL itself requires.

What "Reservation Issues" Actually Cover

Under Airbnb's own policy framework, a covered issue generally includes a host cancelling before check-in, a host failing to provide access to the accommodation, or the accommodation not being reasonably habitable at check-in. When a covered issue is confirmed, the guest is generally entitled to a full or partial refund, or rebooking assistance to a similar property at comparable pricing, subject to availability — the exact outcome depends on the severity of the issue, how much of the stay was affected, and the evidence provided.

What This Means Practically

  • If a host cancels your booking, you're entitled to a refund or remedy under the ACL — that right exists independently of the platform's own cancellation policy wording.
  • Document everything: the original booking confirmation, the cancellation notice, and any communication with the host.
  • Report the issue to the platform promptly (within any stated reporting window) and provide supporting evidence — photos, screenshots, and dated messages all strengthen your claim.
  • Understand that rebooking to an equivalent or better property is a platform courtesy dependent on availability, not a guaranteed legal entitlement — a straightforward refund is the right you can count on regardless.
