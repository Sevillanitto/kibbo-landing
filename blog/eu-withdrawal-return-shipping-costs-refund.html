Return Shipping Costs on a Withdrawal: Who Actually Pays

Share

A seller can only make you pay for return shipping if they told you upfront and in writing — and they can only hold your refund hostage under two specific conditions, not indefinitely.

The Original Delivery Fee Comes Back — Mostly

Exercising your 14-day right of withdrawal under Article 9 of Directive 2011/83/EU triggers a refund obligation covering all payments received, including the original delivery fee, under Article 13(1). There's one carve-out under Article 13(2): if you specifically chose an express or premium delivery option more expensive than the seller's standard offering, the seller only has to refund the equivalent of standard delivery — not the premium you paid for speed.

Who Pays to Send the Item Back

Under Article 14(1), you only bear the direct cost of returning the goods if two conditions are both met: the seller informed you of this obligation in advance and in writing (typically in their terms and conditions, before you completed the purchase), and the seller didn't voluntarily agree to cover the cost themselves. If a seller's terms never actually disclosed that return shipping would be at your cost, the seller bears that cost by default — this is an easy detail for sellers to omit, and it shifts the financial responsibility back to them when they do.

How Fast You Get Your Money — and When It Can Be Held

The seller must refund the full amount within 14 calendar days from the date they were informed of your withdrawal, under Article 13(1). However, Article 13(3) gives the seller a legitimate right to withhold the refund until either they've received the goods back, or you've provided proof you've sent them — a dispatch receipt or tracking confirmation from the postal or courier service. This isn't an open-ended excuse to delay; once you provide that proof, the clock on their obligation to actually pay resumes.

Practical Steps

  1. Check the seller's terms and conditions for whether they actually disclosed a return-shipping cost obligation before you purchased — if they didn't, they owe it.
  2. Keep your dispatch receipt or tracking number for any return — this is exactly what unlocks your refund if the seller is withholding it pending proof of return.
  3. If you chose express delivery originally, expect only the standard delivery equivalent to be refunded, not the premium.
  4. Track the 14-day refund clock from the date you notified the seller of withdrawal, not from when they received the goods back.
  5. If the seller delays beyond what Article 13(3) actually permits once you've provided proof of return, escalate through ECC-Net or your national consumer authority.
