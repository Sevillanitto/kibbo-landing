Titanium Dioxide (E171) in Supplement Capsules: Why the EU Banned It

For years, E171 gave vitamin capsules and tablets their bright, uniform white coating. The EU banned it outright in 2022 — not because harm was proven, but because safety couldn't be confirmed.

What titanium dioxide was used for

Titanium dioxide (E171) is a whitening and opacifying agent used for decades across a huge range of foods and supplements — chewing gum, confectionery, sauces, and very commonly the white coating on vitamin and supplement capsules and tablets, where it gives a clean, uniform, pharmaceutical-looking finish.

Why EFSA changed its position

EFSA had reviewed E171 multiple times, initially raising no safety concerns in 2016. That changed with an updated 2021 evaluation: EFSA's Panel on Food Additives and Flavourings concluded it could not rule out a concern for genotoxicity — the potential for a substance to damage genetic material — based on new data and strengthened assessment methods. Critically, EFSA stated it could not establish a safe daily intake level for the additive at all, which is a stronger regulatory trigger than simply finding a specific harm.

The actual ban timeline

The European Commission's regulation withdrawing authorisation for titanium dioxide as a food additive was adopted on 14 January 2022. Foods produced under the old rules before 7 February 2022 could remain on the market until their best-before or use-by date, giving a phase-out period that extended the practical deadline to around August 2022 for products already in the supply chain.

An important nuance: not every regulator agreed

The EU's ban wasn't universally followed. The US FDA maintains that titanium dioxide is safe as a food additive under current guidance (last substantively reviewed in 1973) and continues to permit it up to 1% of a finished product's weight. The UK and Canada also expressed different views on EFSA's 2021 conclusions. This means titanium dioxide can still legally appear in supplements sold in the US, the UK, and several other markets, even though it's banned in EU-manufactured and EU-sold food products.

How to check a supplement for E171

  1. Check the full ingredient list for "titanium dioxide" or "E171" specifically — it's most often listed among coating or capsule-shell ingredients, not the active formula.
  2. Be more alert with older stock, imported products, or supplements purchased from non-EU retailers shipping into the EU.
  3. If you can't find clear ingredient information, contact the manufacturer directly and ask specifically about E171/titanium dioxide content.
  4. Look for capsules or tablets marketed as reformulated or "E171-free" as a straightforward alternative where available.

What this means practically

  • Titanium dioxide is banned as a food additive across the EU since 2022, specifically due to genotoxicity concerns EFSA couldn't rule out — not a confirmed harm finding, but an unresolved safety question treated as sufficient grounds for a ban.
  • A supplement bought outside the EU, or shipped in from a non-EU seller, may still legally contain E171 under that other country's rules.
  • Checking the ingredient list specifically for "titanium dioxide" or "E171" is the direct way to confirm a capsule's coating status.
  • The additive's continued approval in the US, UK, and elsewhere reflects a genuine, unresolved scientific and regulatory disagreement, not an oversight by either side.
