Banned Pre-Workout Stimulants in Australia: What the Poisons Standard Prohibits

A pre-workout that's a bestseller on a US website can be a scheduled poison in Australia — and four people have died from one specific "fat shredder" ingredient still occasionally found online.

How the Poisons Standard governs sports supplements

The Standard for the Uniform Scheduling of Medicines and Poisons — known as the Poisons Standard or SUSMP — sets out which substances require pharmacy, prescription, or outright prohibited status in Australia. If a sports supplement contains an ingredient scheduled in the Poisons Standard, it's legally treated as a therapeutic good rather than a food, regardless of how it's marketed, and becomes subject to the restrictions that apply to that schedule.

DNP: the deadliest example on record

2,4-Dinitrophenol (DNP), marketed as a "fat shredder," has been linked to at least four confirmed deaths in Australia since 2015, according to NSW Poisons Information Centre data. There is no antidote for DNP poisoning, and even the best available medical care hasn't been enough to save some victims. Following these deaths, DNP was placed in Schedule 10 of the Poisons Standard — the strictest category, banning its use in all circumstances, including clinical research.

Higenamine and DMAA: banned outright in supplements

Higenamine, a beta-2 agonist stimulant, has no history of safe use as a food in Australia and was formally excluded from supplements by a TGA ruling in December 2020. DMAA (1,3-dimethylamylamine), the stimulant once marketed as a "geranium extract" internationally, falls under similar scheduled-substance restrictions preventing its legal sale in Australian sports supplements.

SARMs: a stricter category again

Selective androgen receptor modulators (SARMs), sometimes found even at low contamination levels in muscle-building products, are Schedule 4 — prescription-only medicines — in Australia. Detecting SARMs in a product not explicitly sold as a "SARM" product is a signal of contamination in manufacturing, not a normal or expected ingredient for a mainstream sports supplement.

Why importing these substances is risky, not just difficult

Products containing ingredients from these restricted or prohibited schedules can be legally intercepted by the Australian Border Force when imported, in the same way any other scheduled substance is controlled at the border. Ordering from an overseas retailer doesn't change the legal status of the ingredient once it's entering Australia — the exporting country's rules are irrelevant to how the ABF and TGA treat the shipment.

How to protect yourself

  1. Check any unfamiliar stimulant ingredient by its chemical name against current Poisons Standard schedules before ordering, especially for imported pre-workouts.
  2. Be highly cautious of any product marketed for rapid, dramatic fat loss — DNP-linked deaths were specifically tied to this marketing angle.
  3. Favor products certified by HASTA or Informed Sport, which screen specifically for these scheduled and WADA-prohibited substances.
  4. If a package containing a scheduled substance is seized by the ABF, don't assume it's a mistake — verify the specific ingredient's current Australian legal status before attempting to reorder.

What this means practically

  • DNP has caused real, documented deaths in Australia and is banned outright — treat any "fat shredder" marketing claim with serious suspicion.
  • Higenamine and DMAA are both excluded from legal Australian supplements, regardless of how they're marketed overseas.
  • SARMs found even at low levels in a mainstream product indicate a manufacturing contamination issue, not a normal formulation choice.
  • Import restrictions on these substances are enforced at the Australian border regardless of their legal status in the country you're ordering from.
