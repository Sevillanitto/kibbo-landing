How to Cancel Internet or TV Without Paying an Early Termination Fee

Share

An early termination fee is not automatically illegal because service is slow or you are moving. The contract, disclosures, performance and applicable state or federal rules all matter.

There is no universal federal “poor service cancels the ETF” rule

Cancellation fees are generally governed by the service agreement plus applicable federal and state law. The FTC does not provide a universal rule cancelling every broadband ETF because performance is disputed.

Use the Broadband Consumer Label as evidence

FCC broadband labels disclose key plan information including monthly price, introductory rates, contract requirements, early termination fees and speed metrics. Save the label when you subscribe.

Moving does not automatically erase the fee

A move can matter if the provider cannot serve the new address or the contract contains a relocation provision, but there is no single federal rule making every move a penalty-free cancellation.

Slow speeds create a separate evidence question

Document repeated speed tests against the speed represented by the provider. The FCC complaint form includes a Speed issue category. Whether performance excuses an ETF is a separate contract and consumer-law question.

Challenge the ETF

  1. Save the contract and broadband label.
  2. Record promised speed and price.
  3. Keep dated speed tests.
  4. Document a move or availability issue if relevant.
  5. Ask for the contractual basis and calculation of the fee.

What this means practically

  • Do not rely on a universal federal waiver.
  • Use the label and contract as evidence.
  • Document performance before requesting a waiver.
  • Check state law and provider-specific terms.
