MOT History Isn't a Full Vehicle History: What It Can and Can't Tell You

It's free, it's official, and it's genuinely useful. It's also not the same thing as knowing a car's real history.

What MOT history actually shows

Every test date, pass or fail result, the mileage recorded at each test, and any advisories or failure reasons noted by the tester — going back to whenever the vehicle's first MOT was due (typically its third year on the road). It's a genuinely reliable mileage cross-check, because it's independently recorded at each annual test rather than self-reported by the seller.

What it doesn't show

  • Finance or write-off status — MOT history has no connection to outstanding loans or insurance write-off categories. That requires a separate vehicle history check.
  • Full service and maintenance history — an MOT is a roadworthiness test at a single point in time, not a record of oil changes, timing belt replacements, or general upkeep.
  • Number of previous owners — not tracked by the MOT system at all.
  • Condition between tests — a car can pass its MOT and still develop a serious fault the following week; a pass is a snapshot, not a guarantee of ongoing reliability.

Combine, don't substitute

Treat MOT history as one input among several: a vehicle history check for finance/write-off/theft status, a documented service history from invoices (not just a stamped book, which can be forged), an independent pre-purchase inspection, and the mileage cross-check the MOT record enables. Any single source used alone leaves a real gap.
