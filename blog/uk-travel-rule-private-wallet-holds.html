Travel Rule Holds in the UK: How to Send Crypto to Private Wallets Without Exchange Delays

Your exchange isn't being difficult for no reason — it's legally required to know who owns the wallet you're sending to, and it usually can't verify that automatically.

What the Travel Rule Actually Requires

The Travel Rule implements Financial Action Task Force (FATF) recommendations requiring cryptoasset businesses to collect, verify, and share information about the originator and beneficiary of a crypto transfer above a certain threshold — the same principle applied to traditional wire transfers, adapted for blockchain transactions. UK cryptoasset firms have been required to comply since September 2023.

The practical problem is verification: when you send crypto to your own hardware wallet or another self-hosted wallet, the receiving "institution" is just you — there's no counterparty business on the other end to automatically confirm ownership information the way there is when transferring between two exchanges. Your exchange has to verify this manually, which is exactly where delays and holds come from.

What to Provide to Avoid a Hold

To satisfy your exchange's Travel Rule obligations when withdrawing to a self-hosted wallet, be prepared to provide:

  • Confirmation that you own and control the receiving wallet — most platforms require an explicit declaration of self-hosted wallet ownership, not just the address itself.
  • Proof of ownership where requested — this can include a signed message from the wallet, a screenshot of the wallet interface showing the address, or a small verification transaction, depending on the specific exchange's process.
  • Accurate, consistent naming — if the platform asks you to label the wallet (e.g., "personal hardware wallet"), keep this consistent across future withdrawals to the same address, since inconsistent labeling can itself trigger additional review.

Complete this verification proactively, before you actually need to withdraw urgently — doing it for the first time during a time-sensitive situation is when delays feel most painful.

What This Means for You

If your withdrawal to a private wallet is held, it's very likely a standard Travel Rule verification step, not a sign your account is specifically under suspicion. Check your exchange's specific self-hosted wallet verification process before you need to move funds urgently, and keep the ownership declaration you provide consistent for the same wallet going forward.
