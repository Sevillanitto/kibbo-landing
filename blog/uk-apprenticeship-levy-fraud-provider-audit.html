Apprenticeship Levy Fraud: How to Audit Your Training Provider

A training provider that draws down Levy funds but delivers little real training doesn't just waste your budget — it can trigger a compliance audit of your business too. Here's how to check before that happens.

Who Actually Oversees Levy Spending Now

Apprenticeship Levy funding oversight in England was previously administered by the Education and Skills Funding Agency (ESFA) — but the ESFA formally closed on 31 March 2025, with its functions, including apprenticeship funding administration and provider compliance, absorbed directly into the Department for Education (DfE). If you're researching this topic, be aware that a lot of existing guidance and older articles still reference the ESFA as the active body; contacts, funding agreements, and compliance processes continued essentially unchanged through the DfE's Apprenticeship Service, but the correct current point of contact is the DfE, not a standalone ESFA.

How Levy Fraud Actually Happens

Employers pay into the Apprenticeship Levy and draw down funds through the Apprenticeship Service digital account to pay approved training providers for delivering apprenticeship training. Fraud or poor-value delivery typically takes one of these forms: a provider draws down the full funding for a standard but delivers a fraction of the required off-the-job training hours; a provider enrolls apprentices who don't meet basic eligibility criteria; or a provider claims completion and achievement payments for apprentices who never actually finished or passed.

Because Levy funds are public money, employers who use a provider engaged in this kind of activity can themselves face scrutiny and audit, even if the employer wasn't complicit — the responsibility for genuine delivery doesn't rest solely with the provider.

Auditing Your Training Provider

  1. Confirm the provider is currently listed on the register of approved training providers via the Apprenticeship Service — a provider that's been removed or suspended is an immediate red flag.
  2. Check that the apprentice is actually receiving the statutory minimum 20% off-the-job training time — request evidence of this, not just an assurance.
  3. Request regular progress reviews and evidence of genuine learning activity, not just enrollment confirmation and eventual completion certificates.
  4. Cross-check any completion or achievement claims against your own apprentice's actual account of what training occurred.
  5. Keep your own records of delivery independent of what the provider reports — timesheets, session logs, or manager sign-off on training activity.

If You Suspect a Problem

Report suspected apprenticeship funding misuse or fraud through the Department for Education's current reporting channels — search "report apprenticeship training provider concern" on GOV.UK for the current process, since the specific portal has changed as functions moved from the ESFA into the DfE. Keep your own documentation of what was promised versus what was actually delivered before raising a concern, since this is exactly what an investigation will ask for.
