Buying a Used Car From Another EU Country: The Documents You Need to Verify

Buying a car registered in another member state can be genuinely good value — but every extra step it involves is also an extra place for problems to hide.

Verify the seller, not just the car

A professional trader in another country should be identifiable and traceable — get the full business registration details, not just a phone number and a listing on a marketplace. If it's a private seller, the same identity and ownership checks apply as any private sale, just harder to verify remotely.

The Certificate of Conformity (COC)

The COC confirms the vehicle's technical specification matches EU type-approval standards — you'll typically need it (or an equivalent) to register the car in your own country. Confirm the seller can provide it, or that it's obtainable from the manufacturer, before you commit to the purchase.

VAT status

Whether VAT is due again in your country of registration depends on factors including the vehicle's age and mileage and whether it's classed as "new" under EU VAT rules for means of transport, and whether the original sale was between private individuals or involved a VAT-registered trader. Get this confirmed in writing before paying — an unexpected VAT bill after import is a common and expensive surprise.

Registration, transport and inspection

Plan for: temporary transit documentation or plates to move the car legally, a technical inspection in your home country if required before registration, and the registration process itself, which can take longer and cost more than buying domestically. Get all of this — including who is responsible for transport and any damage in transit — written into the purchase contract.
