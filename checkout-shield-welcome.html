Chrome Extension Free

Checkout Security Shield is installed

It runs entirely on-device, watching checkout and payment pages for things worth a second look before you pay — no network requests, no analytics, nothing collected.

What you'll see

On checkout pages, and on every page

  • Checkout bar (green / orange) — a quick safety summary at the top of checkout pages, green when nothing stood out, orange for hidden extras or added insurance, pre-checked boxes, an unencrypted connection, or a subscription with automatic renewal.
  • Payment gateway badge — a badge, bottom-right: green when a known, trusted gateway (Stripe, PayPal, Redsys, Adyen, Braintree, Bizum, Klarna, Square, and others) is found in the page's own scripts — never from page text or comments — or amber when the checkout asks for card details with no recognized gateway.
  • Lookalike-domain banner — a dismissible banner, never a blocking pop-up, if the domain looks similar to a well-known brand but isn't the same site.
  • Fraud-domain warning — the one warning that can't be dismissed by trusting the site: a red alert if the domain is on a bundled list of sites reported for fraud, with options to go back or continue anyway.
  • Fake-shop signals — an amber note on the checkout bar if the page only offers bank transfer, cryptocurrency, or gift cards with none of the normal payment methods; and, separately in the popup only, a note if no contact, returns, or terms information was found on the page.
  • Grey "third-party scripts" line — purely informational, never a warning: notes script domains on a checkout page that the shield doesn't recognise as first-party, a known gateway, or a common service. It does not inspect what a script does, only whether its domain is recognised.
Using the popup

Click the toolbar icon any time

  • On/off switch — turn the shield off or back on for every site, instantly.
  • Current page status — the hostname, whether it was detected as a checkout page, the gateway found (or "none recognised"), and the full list of findings for that page.
  • "Trust this site" — suppresses the lookalike banner, checkout bar, gateway badge, and third-party line for that site. It is never offered for a domain on the fraud list — that warning can't be trusted away.
  • "Report a false positive" — opens a pre-filled email to let us know if something was flagged that shouldn't have been.
Privacy

Nothing leaves your browser

The extension makes no network requests of its own, has no analytics or telemetry, and collects nothing that leaves your browser. Everything it stores stays on your device, in your browser's local storage:

  • The on/off preference.
  • A local count of how many warnings it has shown on this device.
  • The list of sites you've chosen to trust (capped at 200).
  • Whether you've dismissed the one-time review prompt.
What it can't do

Honest limits

  • It can't verify that a shop is legitimate. Recognising a payment gateway or a common script domain is not the same as the site being safe.
  • The fraud-domain list is a snapshot, refreshed between releases — not a live, real-time feed.
  • A missing warning is not a guarantee that nothing is wrong with a page.
