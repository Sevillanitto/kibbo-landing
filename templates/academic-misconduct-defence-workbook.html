Academic Misconduct Defence Workbook

For students accused of plagiarism, AI misuse, cheating, or collusion — build your case before you sit in front of a panel.

The problem

An accusation is a process, and the process moves fast

A misconduct accusation — whether it's plagiarism, undisclosed AI use, cheating, or collusion — usually comes with a short window to respond and a hearing where you're expected to already have your account straight, your sources organized, and your evidence ready. Going in without a documented timeline or a clear account of what actually happened puts you at a real disadvantage, regardless of whether the accusation itself is fair. This workbook doesn't tell you what to say — it gives you the structure to organize your own defence properly.

Features
  • Timeline to document what happened and when, in order
  • Evidence Inventory and Source Tracker to organize what backs your account
  • AI Usage Disclosure page to clearly document any AI tool use and how
  • Witness Log and Communication Log
  • Hearing Preparation and Question Preparation pages
  • Appeal Tracker if the outcome needs to be challenged afterward
How it works

Step by step

  1. Download the workbook (PDF or Word version included).
  2. Build your Timeline and log every relevant piece of evidence in the Evidence Inventory and Source Tracker.
  3. If AI tool use is part of the accusation, complete the AI Usage Disclosure page honestly and specifically.
  4. Log any witnesses and every communication with staff or the institution.
  5. Use Hearing Preparation and Question Preparation before you attend, and the Appeal Tracker if you need to challenge the outcome.
Who it's for

Any student facing an academic misconduct accusation

Plagiarism, undisclosed or disputed AI tool use, cheating, or collusion allegations — organizing your account and evidence before a hearing or written response is due.
