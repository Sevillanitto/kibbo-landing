The problem

A recall notice rarely tells you what to actually do

A recall or contamination alert names a product, sometimes down to the batch or lot number — but it doesn't tell you what to do once you've confirmed it's yours: whether to stop use immediately, how to document that you did, or what to watch for afterwards. Without a record, it's easy to lose track of exactly when you found out and what you did about it.

This log walks through confirming the match, documenting the actions you take, and keeping a simple health-monitoring note in case symptoms appear later.

Features
  • Batch/Lot Match Confirmation — checks your specific product against the recall or alert notice details
  • Action Log — records what you did (stopped use, contacted the retailer, kept the packaging as evidence) and when
  • Health-Monitoring Note — a simple space to track any symptoms in the days after, even if none appear
How it works

Step by step

  1. Compare your product's batch/lot number against the recall or alert notice to confirm the match.
  2. Stop using the product and log the date and time you did so.
  3. Record every action taken — contacting the retailer or manufacturer, keeping the packaging, requesting a refund.
  4. Use the health-monitoring note to track anything unusual over the following days, and complete an Adverse Reaction Report immediately if symptoms do appear.
Who it's for

Anyone who's just learned a product they own is part of a recall or contamination alert

Built for the moment you find out — before you know whether symptoms will follow — so the response is documented from the start rather than reconstructed later.
