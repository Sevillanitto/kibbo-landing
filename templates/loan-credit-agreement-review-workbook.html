The problem

The advertised rate isn't the number that determines what you pay

Two loan offers with the same headline interest rate can cost meaningfully different amounts once you account for origination fees, the loan term, and any add-on products bundled in. Comparing offers on the rate alone is how people end up choosing the more expensive option without realizing it — the math that actually matters isn't the one lenders put in the largest font.

This workbook takes the real terms from each offer and calculates the total cost of credit automatically, so the comparison is based on what you'll actually pay, not just what's advertised.

Features
  • Side-by-Side Offer Comparison — enter multiple offers and compare them in one view
  • Auto-Calculating Total Cost of Credit — the real number behind the advertised rate
  • Rate vs. Total Cost Breakdown — see exactly where the gap between the two comes from
  • Red Flags Checklist — terms worth a second look before you sign anything
How it works

Step by step

  1. Download the Excel workbook and enter the terms of each loan or credit offer you're considering.
  2. Let the workbook calculate the real total cost of credit for each one automatically.
  3. Compare offers side by side using the total cost, not just the advertised rate.
  4. Run each offer against the Red Flags Checklist before you commit to anything.
  5. Keep the completed comparison as your own record of what you were actually offered.
Who it's for

Anyone comparing more than one loan or credit offer

Whether it's a personal loan, a car loan, or a credit line, the offer with the lowest advertised rate isn't always the cheapest one once fees and term length are actually accounted for.
