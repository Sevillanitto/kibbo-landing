Online Course Refund Toolkit

When the course doesn't match what was advertised — build the case for a refund, not just a complaint.

The problem

"It wasn't what the sales page said" needs to be provable

Online course platforms and instructors don't refund on a general sense of disappointment — a request that lays out exactly what was advertised against what was actually delivered, with dates and screenshots, gets treated very differently than a vague "this wasn't good." This toolkit builds that comparison and tracks the request through to a resolution, or a chargeback if it isn't one.

Features
  • Purchase Details page to record exactly what and when you bought
  • Advertised Promises checklist to capture the sales page's specific claims
  • Course Differences log — advertised vs. actually delivered
  • Communication Log and Refund Timeline
  • Chargeback Notes if the platform or instructor doesn't refund directly
How it works

Step by step

  1. Download the toolkit (PDF or Excel version included).
  2. Fill in Purchase Details and work through the Advertised Promises checklist while the sales page is still up — screenshot it.
  3. Log every Course Difference you find between what was promised and what you actually got.
  4. Request the refund, logging it in the Communication Log and Refund Timeline.
  5. If the platform refuses or goes quiet, use the Chargeback Notes to prepare a card-issuer dispute.
Who it's for

Anyone disputing an online course purchase

A course that doesn't match its sales page — missing content, an instructor who never appears, outdated material sold as current — and the refund request or chargeback that follows.
