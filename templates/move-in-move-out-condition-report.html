Move-In / Move-Out Condition Report

The single biggest factor in a deposit dispute is whether you documented the property's condition before you moved in — and whether you can prove it. A room-by-room record, done properly, ends the argument before it starts.

The problem

"It was already like that" only works if you can prove it

Most deposit disputes come down to a single question: was the damage or wear already there when you moved in, or did it happen on your watch? Without a dated, room-by-room record made at the start of the tenancy — and another one at the end, in the same format — that question has no answer except whoever's more persuasive in writing. Landlords who inspect thoroughly at move-out and loosely (or not at all) at move-in have a structural advantage in that argument, unless you closed the gap yourself.

Features
  • Room-by-Room Condition Log — a consistent format used at both move-in and move-out
  • Photo/Video Log — a place to reference exactly which images correspond to which room and date
  • Existing Damage & Wear Notes — recorded at move-in so it can't later be attributed to you
  • Signature Section — for you and, ideally, the landlord or agent to sign off on the record
How it works

Step by step

  1. Download the report and complete it room by room on move-in day, before unpacking.
  2. Photograph or video each room as you go, and note the file names or timestamps in the Photo/Video Log.
  3. Record any existing damage or wear explicitly, however minor — this is what protects you at move-out.
  4. Get the report signed if the landlord or agent is present, or send a copy by email the same day if not.
  5. Repeat the exact same process at move-out, using a fresh copy, for a direct before-and-after comparison.
Who it's for

Any tenant starting or ending a tenancy

Whether you're moving in and want to protect your deposit from day one, or moving out and need a clean before-and-after record, the same format works for both — that consistency is what makes the comparison credible.
