Lease Violation Notice Log

If a lease violation ever ends up in front of a judge, a consistent, dated record of the notice you gave and the cure period you allowed is what makes the case — not your recollection of events months later.

The problem

An informal warning is easy to dispute later — a logged notice with a cure period isn't

Most jurisdictions require a landlord to give formal, specific notice of a lease violation and a defined cure period before further action — like non-renewal or eviction — is available. A text message or a verbal warning that was never logged consistently is difficult to rely on later, and inconsistent recordkeeping across units or tenants is exactly what an unfair-treatment claim looks for. A single, consistent log across every unit is what makes each notice defensible on its own.

Features
  • Violation Log — the specific lease clause violated, the unit, and the date observed
  • Notice Issued Tracker — how and when notice was delivered to the tenant
  • Cure Period Tracker — the deadline given to correct the violation, and whether it was met
  • Multi-Unit View — track violations and notices consistently across every property you manage
How it works

Step by step

  1. Download the log (PDF or Excel version included).
  2. Record each violation as it's observed — the specific clause, the unit, and the date.
  3. Log how and when notice was delivered to the tenant in the Notice Issued Tracker.
  4. Set the cure period deadline and track whether the violation was corrected in time.
  5. Keep every unit's history in the same log, so your recordkeeping is consistent across your portfolio.
Who it's for

Landlords and property managers

Whether you manage a single rental or a portfolio of units, a consistent, dated notice record protects you if a lease enforcement decision is ever challenged.
