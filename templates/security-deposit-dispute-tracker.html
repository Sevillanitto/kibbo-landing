Security Deposit Dispute Tracker

A withheld deposit is a paperwork fight — the tenant with the clearer record, not the louder complaint, usually wins it. Log every deduction, every message, and every deadline in one place before you respond.

The problem

A landlord's deduction list is easy to write and hard to dispute — unless you kept your own record

A security deposit deduction letter usually arrives itemized and confident-sounding, with no independent way for you to check it against what was actually agreed or actually damaged. Without your own log of what the deductions claim, what you actually communicated with the landlord, and what evidence you can point to, a dispute becomes your word against theirs — and theirs came first, in writing.

This tracker builds that record before you send a single reply: every deduction the landlord claims, every message exchanged, the evidence backing your side, and the deadline you're working against.

Features
  • Deduction Log — each claimed deduction, the amount, and whether you dispute it
  • Communication Log — every message with the landlord or agency, dated
  • Evidence Checklist — move-in/move-out photos, receipts, and inspection reports
  • Escalation Timeline — the deadline to respond and the next step if it's missed
How it works

Step by step

  1. Download the tracker (PDF or Excel version included).
  2. Enter each deduction the landlord has claimed in the Deduction Log, and mark whether you agree or dispute it.
  3. Log every message exchanged with the landlord or agency in the Communication Log as it happens.
  4. Work through the Evidence Checklist to confirm what you actually have to back your side.
  5. Use the Escalation Timeline to track your response deadline and what comes next if the dispute isn't resolved directly.
Who it's for

Any tenant disputing a withheld or reduced deposit

Whether the landlord is deducting for damage you believe is normal wear, cleaning you believe was already done, or a charge with no explanation at all — the tracker keeps the dispute organized from the first deduction claim to resolution.
