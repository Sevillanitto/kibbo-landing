The problem

Non-compete clauses are dense, and enforceability varies by state

Enforceability swings wildly depending on which state's law applies — some states ban them outright, others enforce them only if the terms are "reasonable," and what counts as reasonable is never spelled out in a fixed formula. This worksheet doesn't tell you whether your clause is enforceable — no template honestly can — but it gives you one place to extract the exact wording that matters (duration, geographic scope, restricted activity, compensation offered) and flag anything that looks disproportionate, so you walk into a conversation with a lawyer or your state labor office already knowing what to ask.

Features
  • Extracts the 3 things that actually matter: duration, scope, restricted activity
  • Red flags checklist for disproportionate or unusual clauses
  • Space for your own questions before consulting a lawyer
  • Instant download, editable PDF/Word
How it works

Step by step

  1. Download the template (PDF or Word version included).
  2. Copy the exact wording of your clause into the Clause Analysis page — don't paraphrase.
  3. Work through the Red Flags Checklist.
  4. Use the Notes & Next Steps page to prepare your questions before consulting a lawyer.
Who it's for

Anyone asked to sign — or already bound by — a non-compete clause

Employees or new hires who want to understand exactly what their clause requires before agreeing to it, or before deciding whether to push back.
