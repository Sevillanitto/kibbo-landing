The problem

Airlines rely on you not remembering exactly what you were told

A flight-disruption claim rarely gets resolved in one phone call. It's a string of conversations — a gate agent, a call centre, a live chat, an email — each one a chance for the story to shift, a promised callback to never happen, or a reference number to get lost in the noise. Without a written record, it's your word against a company that handles thousands of these calls a day.

Logging every contact the moment it happens — who, when, what channel, what was actually said, and what happens next — is what turns a vague "they told me they'd sort it" into a documented pattern of broken promises an ombudsman or small-claims judge can actually follow.

How it works

Step by step

  1. Download the PDF pack and the Excel version as soon as you make first contact with the airline.
  2. Log each conversation: date, contact person, channel used, what was said, and the next step agreed.
  3. Record every reference or complaint ID you're given, so nothing gets lost between calls.
  4. Add any promise made — a callback, a refund timeline, a compensation offer — to the separate promises-and-follow-up tracker with its own deadline.
  5. Use the log as your evidence trail if the airline stalls or contradicts itself later.
Who it's for

Anyone in a multi-contact dispute with an airline

A delay, cancellation, or baggage claim that drags across several calls and emails — the log is what keeps the airline's own promises on record, in their words, not yours.
