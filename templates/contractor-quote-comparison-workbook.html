The problem

The lowest number on the page isn't always the lowest cost

One quote bundles permits into the total, another lists them as "TBD." One includes a 2-year warranty, another says nothing about warranty at all. Laid out separately, on paper, in different formats, it's genuinely hard to tell which contractor is actually offering the better deal — not just the better-looking number.

This workbook puts every quote through the same categories, so the comparison is apples to apples instead of whichever quote happened to look tidiest.

Features
  • Side-by-Side Comparison Grid — labor, materials, permits, timeline, and warranty terms for every quote, in the same rows
  • Hidden Cost Flags — a dedicated row for costs quotes often bury or omit (permits, disposal, change-order rates)
  • Automatic Lowest-Cost Summary — the workbook calculates the actual lowest total once every category is filled in
  • Lowest-Cost-Per-Day Summary — factors in the quoted timeline, so a cheaper-but-slower quote doesn't automatically win
How it works

Step by step

  1. Enter each contractor's quote into its own column, using the same category rows for every quote.
  2. Fill in the hidden-cost flags for anything a quote left vague or excluded.
  3. Let the workbook calculate the lowest total cost automatically.
  4. Check the lowest-cost-per-day figure before deciding — a faster job can be worth a higher headline price.
Who it's for

Anyone getting multiple quotes before hiring a contractor

Works for any renovation or home service job where you're comparing more than one bid — kitchens, roofing, extensions, or a single-room repair.
