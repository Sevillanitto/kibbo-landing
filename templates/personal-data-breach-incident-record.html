The problem

A breach notice today, a fraud attempt months later

A breach notification tells you something happened, but the real risk often shows up weeks or months afterward — a delayed fraud attempt, a phishing email that references the exposed data, a login you don't recognize. If you can't point back to exactly what was disclosed, when you found out, and what you did in response, it becomes much harder to prove the connection or hold the company to what it promised.

This record keeps the exact breach notice, the data categories the company says were exposed, every protective action you took — password changes, fraud alerts, credit freezes — and an ongoing log for monitoring, so the full picture exists in one place from the day you were notified onward.

Features
  • Breach Notification Record — the exact notice, date received, and date the company says it occurred
  • Data Categories Exposed — what the company disclosed was actually involved
  • Actions Taken Log — password changes, two-factor authentication, fraud alerts, credit freezes
  • Ongoing Monitoring Log — for tracking suspicious activity in the weeks and months after
  • Company Response Tracker — remediation offered, and whether it was followed through
How it works

Step by step

  1. Download the record and save the original breach notification exactly as received.
  2. Log the specific data categories the company says were affected.
  3. Record every protective action you take, with the date.
  4. Keep the ongoing monitoring log updated as you check statements and accounts over time.
  5. Track any remediation, identity-protection service, or reimbursement the company offers.
Who it's for

Anyone notified that their personal data may have been exposed in a breach

Whether the risk turns into actual fraud or not, this keeps the notification, your response, and your ongoing monitoring documented from day one.
