The problem

A cross-border complaint fails when it's sent down the wrong channel

A purchase from a seller in another country doesn't automatically fall under the same complaint process as a domestic one — sometimes a cross-border network like ECC-Net applies, sometimes an ADR (alternative dispute resolution) scheme does, and sometimes neither does and you're left with the seller's own process or small claims. Filing through the wrong channel wastes time you don't get back. This pack is a preparation tool, not a finished complaint letter — it helps you work out which rules actually apply, gather what you'll need, and track where the complaint has gone.

Features
  • Purchase & Parties Details — seller location, your location, purchase date and value
  • Applicable Rules guide — working out whether ECC-Net, an ADR scheme, or neither applies to your situation
  • Evidence Checklist
  • Complaint Routing Tracker — where you've filed and what's pending
How it works

Step by step

  1. Download the pack (PDF or Word version included).
  2. Fill in Purchase & Parties Details for your specific transaction.
  3. Work through the Applicable Rules guide to identify whether ECC-Net, an ADR scheme, or neither applies to your case.
  4. Complete the Evidence Checklist before you file anywhere.
  5. Log every complaint you submit in the Complaint Routing Tracker, so you know exactly where things stand.

Anyone disputing a purchase from a seller in another country

A cross-border online purchase where it isn't obvious which complaint process — ECC-Net, an ADR scheme, or neither — actually applies to your situation.
