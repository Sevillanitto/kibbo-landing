Refund for a Defective Product

Know your rights, the deadlines that apply, and the free tools to act on it.

Last verified: 13 October 2026

US UK Europe

Can you claim?

Does this apply where you are?

US: No general federal right to return/refund goods — the Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act (15 U.S.C. 2301 et seq.) only regulates written warranties where a business chooses to offer one; it does not require a refund or return policy.
UK: Consumer Rights Act 2015, Part 1 Chapter 2
Europe: Consumer Rights Directive 2011/83/EU (withdrawal right) and Directive (EU) 2019/771 (sale of goods legal guarantee)

Your rights

No general federal right to return/refund goods — the Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act (15 U.S.C. 2301 et seq.) only regulates written warranties where a business chooses to offer one; it does not require a refund or return policy.

Warranty rules
Magnuson-Moss does not require any business to provide a written warranty. Where a written warranty is offered, the business cannot disclaim or modify implied warranties, and a 'full warranty' designation entitles the customer to a replacement or refund if repair isn't possible. Remedies otherwise depend on the seller's own return policy and state consumer-protection/UCC implied-warranty law.

Consumer Rights Act 2015, Part 1 Chapter 2

Short term right to reject
A 30-day window from delivery/taking possession of goods that don't conform to the contract, within which the consumer can reject them for a full refund.
Six month presumption
If the consumer chooses repair, replacement, price reduction or the final right to reject within the first 6 months, the fault is presumed to have been present at delivery unless the trader proves otherwise (or this is inconsistent with the circumstances, e.g. obvious abuse/misuse). No deduction can be made for use where goods are rejected within the first 6 months, except for motor vehicles.
After six months
The burden of proof shifts to the consumer to show the defect was present at delivery.

Consumer Rights Directive 2011/83/EU (withdrawal right) and Directive (EU) 2019/771 (sale of goods legal guarantee)

Withdrawal right
Article 9(1) of Directive 2011/83/EU: for distance/off-premises contracts, "the consumer shall have a period of 14 days" to withdraw from the contract without giving any reason.
Legal guarantee
Article 10 of Directive (EU) 2019/771 ('Liability of the seller') makes the seller liable where a lack of conformity becomes apparent within two years of delivery (member states may set a longer national period).

Evidence to gather

  • Order confirmation and receipt
  • Photos/video of the fault
  • Seller messages
  • Proof of return

Steps

  1. Gather your evidence (see the list above).
  2. Send a written complaint to the business or provider, citing the rule for your region from "Your rights" above.
  3. If unresolved by their deadline, escalate using the route named in "Your rights" for your region.
  4. If still unresolved, consider your region's ombudsman, regulator, or small claims court — see "Your rights" for the specific option.
