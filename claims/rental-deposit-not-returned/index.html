Rental Deposit Not Returned

Know your rights, the deadlines that apply, and the free tools to act on it.

Last verified: 14 October 2026

US UK

Can you claim?

Does this apply where you are?

US: State landlord-tenant statutes (no federal deposit-return deadline); small claims court is the standard enforcement route in every state for amounts within that state's small-claims limit.
UK: Tenancy Deposit Protection (Housing Act 2004 Part 6 Chapter 4, as amended) — England and Wales assured shorthold tenancies starting after 6 April 2007

Your rights

State landlord-tenant statutes (no federal deposit-return deadline); small claims court is the standard enforcement route in every state for amounts within that state's small-claims limit.

If a landlord doesn't return the deposit or an itemized deduction statement within the state deadline, tenants in every state can sue in their local small claims court; limits and procedures vary by state.

Tenancy Deposit Protection (Housing Act 2004 Part 6 Chapter 4, as amended) — England and Wales assured shorthold tenancies starting after 6 April 2007

Protection deadline
The landlord (or letting agent) must put the deposit in a government-backed tenancy deposit protection (TDP) scheme within 30 days of receiving it.
Return deadline
The deposit must be returned to the tenant within 10 days of landlord and tenant agreeing how much they'll get back.
Penalty for not protecting
Under Housing Act 2004 s.214 (as amended), if the landlord failed to protect the deposit or give the required prescribed information, a court must order the landlord to pay the tenant a sum "not less than the amount of the deposit and not more than three times the amount of the deposit", within 14 days of the court order.
Dispute route
If the deposit is protected in an 'insured' scheme and there's a dispute, the landlord/agent must give the deposit to the TDP scheme, which holds it until the dispute is settled via the scheme's free alternative dispute resolution service.

Evidence to gather

  • Tenancy agreement
  • Proof of deposit payment and protection certificate
  • Move-in and move-out condition photos
  • Inventory report
  • Messages with landlord

Steps

  1. Gather your evidence (see the list above).
  2. Send a written complaint to the business or provider, citing the rule for your region from "Your rights" above.
  3. If unresolved by their deadline, escalate using the route named in "Your rights" for your region.
  4. If still unresolved, consider your region's ombudsman, regulator, or small claims court — see "Your rights" for the specific option.
