Final Paycheck Not Paid

Know your rights, the deadlines that apply, and the free tools to act on it.

Last verified: 14 October 2026

US UK

Can you claim?

Does this apply where you are?

US: No federal final-paycheck deadline — governed entirely by state law, which varies.
UK: Employment Rights Act 1996 (notice pay) and the statutory holiday entitlement rules (Working Time Regulations 1998, as reflected in current gov.uk guidance)

Your rights

No federal final-paycheck deadline — governed entirely by state law, which varies.

StateDetails
CaliforniaQuit deadline: Immediately if the employee gave at least 72 hours' notice and quits on the day stated; otherwise within 72 hours of quitting.
Fired deadline: Immediately at the time of termination.
Vacation payout: Accrued unused vacation must be paid out in all separation scenarios.		Source →
ArizonaQuit deadline: No later than the regular payday for the pay period during which the termination occurred.
Fired deadline: Within seven working days or the end of the next regular pay period, whichever is sooner.
Vacation payout: Not specified by statute.		Source →
MinnesotaQuit deadline: Not later than the first regularly scheduled payday following the employee's final day of employment (may extend to the second payday, but never more than 20 calendar days total, if the first payday is fewer than 5 days after termination).
Fired deadline: Immediately due and payable upon the employee's demand; the employer is in default if not paid within 24 hours of demand.
Vacation payout: Not specified by statute.		Source →
MissouriQuit deadline: Not specified; the statute addresses discharge (being fired) only — see fired_deadline.
Fired deadline: Wages are due on the day of discharge; if not paid within 7 days of the employee's written demand, wages continue accruing at the same rate until paid, up to a maximum of 60 days.
Vacation payout: Not specified by statute.		Source →
AlabamaNot yet verified
AlaskaQuit deadline: If you quit, you must be paid by the next regular payday that is at least three working days after your last day worked.
Fired deadline: If terminated by the employer, your employer must pay you all monies owed within three working days after the day of termination (not counting weekends and holidays).
Vacation payout: An employer only has to pay vacation/PTO benefits if it has a policy to pay such benefits, or has made a promise or has a contract to pay them; the Department enforces an employer's own rules for these kinds of payments.		Source →
ArkansasQuit deadline: Not specified; the statute addresses discharge only — see fired_deadline.
Fired deadline: An employer that discharges an employee is required to pay all wages due by the next regular payday. An employer that fails to make the payment within seven (7) days of the next regular payday owes the employee double the wages due.
Vacation payout: Not specified by statute.		Source →
ColoradoQuit deadline: Due and payable on the next regular payday.
Fired deadline: Due and payable immediately. If the employer's payroll unit isn't operating at that moment, wages must be made available no later than six hours after the start of its next regular workday — or 24 hours if the payroll unit is located off the work site.
Vacation payout: If an employer provides paid vacation, it must pay out, upon separation, all vacation pay earned and determinable under the terms of any agreement between employer and employee.		Source →
ConnecticutQuit deadline: Not later than the next regular payday.
Fired deadline: Not later than the business day next succeeding the date of discharge.
Vacation payout: Not specified by statute.		Source →
DelawareQuit deadline: Due on the later of: the next date wages would normally be paid under the employer's regular pay cycle, or three business days after the last day worked.
Fired deadline: Same rule as quitting — due on the later of the next regular payday or three business days after the last day worked; applies whether the employee quit, resigned, was discharged, suspended, or laid off.
Vacation payout: Not specified by statute for separation specifically; employers must post their general vacation pay policy under a separate provision (§ 1108).		Source →
FloridaQuit deadline: No state-law deadline — confirmed via the full section index of Florida Statutes Chapter 448, Part I (§§ 448.01–448.111), none of which sets a final-wage payment deadline. Pay by the next regular payday under the federal FLSA default.
Fired deadline: Same as quit_deadline — no state statute; next regular payday under the federal FLSA default.
Vacation payout: Not specified by statute.		Source →
GeorgiaQuit deadline: No state-law deadline — the Georgia Department of Labor's own official guidance on unpaid final wages cites only the federal Fair Labor Standards Act (payment of at least minimum wage for all hours worked, plus overtime) and directs workers to file a wage claim in small claims (Magistrate) court; it names no state-specific final-pay deadline.
Fired deadline: Same as quit_deadline — no state-law deadline; the federal FLSA standard and small-claims court are the only remedies the state's own guidance cites.
Vacation payout: Not specified by statute.		Source →
HawaiiQuit deadline: Due by the next regular payday. If the employee gave at least one pay period's notice of intent to quit, all wages earned are due at the time of quitting.
Fired deadline: Due in full at the time of discharge, or no later than the next working day if immediate payment isn't possible.
Vacation payout: Not specified by statute.		Source →
IdahoNot yet verified
IllinoisQuit deadline: At the time of separation, if possible, but in no event later than the next regularly scheduled payday.
Fired deadline: At the time of separation, if possible, but in no event later than the next regularly scheduled payday — Illinois draws no distinction between quitting and being discharged.
Vacation payout: Final compensation includes the monetary equivalent of earned, unused vacation and holidays. An employee is not entitled to severance, sick pay, or holiday pay at separation unless the employer promised it in an employment contract or other agreement.		Source →
IndianaQuit deadline: No later than the next regularly scheduled payday on which the employee would have been normally paid had employment continued.
Fired deadline: No later than the next regularly scheduled payday on which the employee would have been normally paid had employment continued — the same deadline applies whether the employee quit or was discharged.
Vacation payout: Accrued vacation pay is considered a form of compensation; an employee may be entitled to a pro rata share of accrued vacation at the time of termination, subject to any conditions set in the employer's policy or contract.		Source →
IowaQuit deadline: Not later than the next regular payday for the pay period in which the wages were earned; Iowa law does not distinguish between quitting and being discharged.
Fired deadline: Not later than the next regular payday for the pay period in which the wages were earned — same rule as quitting.
Vacation payout: Owed only if due under an agreement with the employer or an employer policy establishing pro rata vacation accrual; if so, the final increment must be in proportion to the fraction of the year the employee actually worked.		Source →
KansasQuit deadline: Not later than the next regular payday upon which the employee would have been paid if still employed.
Fired deadline: Not later than the next regular payday upon which the employee would have been paid if still employed — the same deadline applies to discharge and voluntary quit.
Vacation payout: Not specified directly by statute; Kansas case law has recognized accrued vacation as earned wages owed at separation, depending on the employer's contract or policy terms.		Source →
KentuckyQuit deadline: By the next normal pay period following separation, or 14 days following separation, whichever is later.
Fired deadline: By the next normal pay period following separation, or 14 days following separation, whichever is later — the statute applies the identical rule to dismissal and voluntary leaving.
Vacation payout: Vested vacation pay counts as wages. Whether a vacation balance has "vested" turns on the employer's own policy.		Source →
LouisianaQuit deadline: On or before the next regular payday for the pay cycle during which the employee was working at the time of separation, or no later than fifteen days following the date of resignation, whichever occurs first.
Fired deadline: On or before the next regular payday, or no later than fifteen days following the date of discharge, whichever occurs first.
Vacation payout: Accrued vacation counts as an amount then due only if the employee had accrued the right to paid vacation under the employer's policy and had not already taken or been compensated for it; an employer cannot forfeit vacation pay actually earned under its own policy.		Source →
MaineQuit deadline: No later than the employee's next established payday; Maine law does not distinguish between quitting and being discharged.
Fired deadline: No later than the employee's next established payday — same rule as quitting.
Vacation payout: All unused paid vacation accrued under the employer's policy on or after 1 January 2023 must be paid out at separation, unless the employee works for an employer with 10 or fewer employees or for a public employer, which are exempt from this requirement.		Source →
MarylandQuit deadline: On or before the day on which the employee would have been paid the wages if employment had not terminated (i.e., the next regular payday).
Fired deadline: On or before the day on which the employee would have been paid the wages if employment had not terminated — the same rule applies whether the employee quit or was discharged.
Vacation payout: Owed as cash unless the employer has a written policy, communicated to the employee, stating that unused vacation is forfeited at termination; without such a policy, the employee is entitled to the cash value of unused earned vacation leave.		Source →
MassachusettsQuit deadline: On the following regular payday or, in the absence of a regular payday, on the following Saturday.
Fired deadline: On the day of discharge.
Vacation payout: "Wages" under the Massachusetts Wage Act includes any holiday or vacation payments due an employee under an oral or written agreement, so accrued vacation is owed on the same deadline as regular wages.		Source →
MichiganQuit deadline: As soon as the amount can, with due diligence, be determined.
Fired deadline: Immediately — as soon as the amount can, with due diligence, be determined.
Vacation payout: "Fringe benefits," which include vacation, must be paid in accordance with the terms of the employer's written contract or written policy; they are owed only if that contract or policy provides for them.		Source →
MississippiNot yet verified
MontanaQuit deadline: All unpaid wages are due and payable on the next regular payday for the pay period during which the employee was separated, or 15 days from the date of separation, whichever occurs first.
Fired deadline: All unpaid wages are due and payable immediately upon separation, unless the employer has a written personnel policy that extends the time for payment to the next regular payday for the pay period or to within 15 days of separation, whichever occurs first.
Vacation payout: Not specified by statute.		Source →
NebraskaQuit deadline: Final wages are due on the next regular payday or within two weeks of the termination date, whichever is sooner — the same rule applies whether the employee quit or was discharged.
Fired deadline: Final wages are due on the next regular payday or within two weeks of the termination date, whichever is sooner — the same rule applies whether the employee quit or was discharged.
Vacation payout: All earned, unused vacation and PTO must be paid out with final wages; there is no exception.		Source →
NevadaQuit deadline: No later than the day the employee would have regularly been paid, or 7 days after resigning or quitting, whichever is earlier.
Fired deadline: Wages and compensation earned and unpaid at the time of discharge become due and payable immediately.
Vacation payout: Not specified by statute; depends on the employer's own written policy.		Source →
New HampshireQuit deadline: Due on the next regular payday if the employee quits without giving at least one pay period's notice; within 72 hours if the employee gave at least one pay period's notice of intent to quit.
Fired deadline: Due in full within 72 hours of discharge.
Vacation payout: Vacation pay, like severance pay, personal days, holiday pay, sick pay, and reimbursable expenses, is considered wages "when due" if it is a matter of the employer's practice or policy — meaning promised, accrued vacation must be paid out on the same timeline as other wages.		Source →
New JerseyQuit deadline: No later than the regular payday for the pay period during which the separation occurred — the same deadline applies whether the employee was discharged, laid off, suspended due to a labor dispute, or quit/resigned for any reason.
Fired deadline: No later than the regular payday for the pay period during which the separation occurred — the same deadline applies whether the employee was discharged, laid off, suspended due to a labor dispute, or quit/resigned for any reason.
Vacation payout: Not specified by statute.		Source →
New MexicoQuit deadline: Due on the next succeeding regular payday, unless the employee has a written contract for a definite period of employment.
Fired deadline: If the unpaid wages are a "fixed and definite amount" (hourly wages, salary, day rate, or accrued unused vacation pay) and the employee demands payment, due within 5 days of discharge; otherwise (task, piece-rate, or commission wages), due within 10 days of discharge.
Vacation payout: Accrued, unused vacation pay is treated as a "fixed and definite" wage amount and must be paid within 5 days of discharge on the employee's demand, per the state's official guidance interpreting NMSA § 50-4-4.		Source →
New YorkQuit deadline: No later than the regular payday for the pay period during which the termination occurred — the same deadline applies whether the employee quit or was discharged.
Fired deadline: No later than the regular payday for the pay period during which the termination occurred — the same deadline applies whether the employee quit or was discharged.
Vacation payout: Not specified by statute.		Source →
North CarolinaQuit deadline: All wages due must be paid on or before the next regular payday, through the regular pay channels or by mail if the employee requests it — the same rule applies whether the employee quit or was discharged.
Fired deadline: All wages due must be paid on or before the next regular payday, through the regular pay channels or by mail if the employee requests it — the same rule applies whether the employee quit or was discharged.
Vacation payout: Earned vacation pay cannot be forfeited unless the employer has a written forfeiture clause in its vacation, commission, bonus, or termination policy — and even then, whether it's actually forfeited depends on the exact wording of that clause and the reason for separation.		Source →
North DakotaQuit deadline: Due on the regular payday established in advance by the employer for the period worked — the same rule applies whether the employee quit or was discharged.
Fired deadline: Due on the regular payday established in advance by the employer for the period worked; if paid by certified mail, the employer must send it to the address the employee designated or one otherwise agreed upon (this certified-mail requirement doesn't apply to voluntary quits).
Vacation payout: Not specified by statute.		Source →
OhioQuit deadline: No separate rule for quitting — final wages follow the same semimonthly schedule required for all wages under R.C. 4113.15: wages earned in the first half of a month are due on or before the 1st of the next month, and wages earned in the second half are due on or before the 15th.
Fired deadline: Same semimonthly schedule as for a resignation (R.C. 4113.15) — Ohio does not set a faster deadline for discharge. If wages remain unpaid 30 days beyond the regular payday, the employer is additionally liable for liquidated damages equal to 6% of the unpaid amount or $200, whichever is greater.
Vacation payout: Not specified by statute.		Source →
OklahomaQuit deadline: Wages are due in full at the next regular designated payday for the pay period in which the work was performed (40 O.S. § 165.3) — the same deadline applies whether the employee quit or was discharged.
Fired deadline: Same next-regular-payday deadline as a resignation (40 O.S. § 165.3). A willful failure to pay wages not subject to a bona fide disagreement makes the employer additionally liable for liquidated damages of 2% of the unpaid wages per day the failure continues.
Vacation payout: Vacation pay counts as "wages" only if it is agreed upon between employer and employee or provided for in the employer's established policy (40 O.S. § 165.1); Oklahoma does not independently require vacation payout absent such an agreement or policy.		Source →
OregonQuit deadline: If the employee gives at least 48 hours' notice (excluding weekends/holidays), the final check is due on the last day worked (or the next business day if that day falls on a weekend/holiday). With less than 48 hours' notice, wages are due within 5 business days or on the next regular payday, whichever comes first.
Fired deadline: Due by the end of the next business day after discharge.
Vacation payout: Not mandatory by statute, but the employer must follow its own established policy or agreement on payment of accrued vacation or severance pay at termination; if such a policy exists and the worker qualifies, the accrued amount must be paid at separation.		Source →
PennsylvaniaQuit deadline: Wages become due and payable no later than the next regular payday on which they would otherwise be due; if requested by the employee, payment must be made by certified mail.
Fired deadline: Same next-regular-payday rule as a resignation — the Wage Payment and Collection Law does not set a separate, faster deadline for discharge.
Vacation payout: Not an independent entitlement, but "fringe benefits or wage supplements" (including vacation, holiday and separation pay) count as wages under the Wage Payment and Collection Law if due under an employee benefit plan or agreement with the employer, and must then be paid within 10 days after payment is required, or within 60 days after the employee files a proper claim.		Source →
Rhode IslandQuit deadline: Wages are due in full (or prorated) with all other wages on the next regular payday following separation — the same rule applies whether the employee quit or was discharged. If the separation is due to the employer liquidating, merging, disposing of, or moving the business out of state, all wages become due within 24 hours of separation.
Fired deadline: Same next-regular-payday rule as a resignation (R.I. Gen. Laws § 28-14-4), except the 24-hour rule above for business liquidation/closure separations.
Vacation payout: If the employee has completed at least one year of service, any vacation pay accrued or awarded under a collective bargaining agreement, written or verbal company policy, or other agreement becomes wages and must be paid in full or on a prorated basis with the final wages.		Source →
South CarolinaQuit deadline: All wages due must be paid within 48 hours of the time of separation or by the next regular payday, which may not exceed 30 days — the same rule applies regardless of why the employee separated.
Fired deadline: Same 48-hours-or-next-payday (max 30 days) rule as a resignation (S.C. Code § 41-10-50).
Vacation payout: "Wages" is defined (§ 41-10-10(2)) to include vacation, holiday, and sick leave payments due under any employer policy or employment contract — so accrued vacation must be paid out at separation only if the employer's own policy or contract makes it payable.		Source →
South DakotaQuit deadline: Unpaid wages are due and payable not later than the next regular stated payday for the pay period in which those hours would normally have been paid, or as soon after that as the employee returns all of the employer's property in the employee's possession.
Fired deadline: Same next-regular-payday-or-return-of-property rule as a resignation (SDCL 60-11-10).
Vacation payout: Not specified by statute — South Dakota has no law requiring payout of accrued vacation at separation; a written policy or agreement that promises a payout is enforceable, and one that says unused vacation is forfeited is equally enforceable.		Source →
TennesseeQuit deadline: All wages or salary earned must be paid in full no later than the next regular payday following the date of voluntary leaving, or 21 days following that date, whichever occurs last.
Fired deadline: Same next-payday-or-21-days (whichever is later) rule as a resignation (Tenn. Code Ann. § 50-2-103(g)) — applies equally to dismissal.
Vacation payout: Not required by statute — an employer is only required to pay out accrued but unused vacation time if its own written policy or labor agreement specifically requires compensation of unused vacation pay or other compensatory time upon separation.		Source →
TexasQuit deadline: An employer must pay in full an employee who leaves employment other than by discharge no later than the next regularly scheduled payday.
Fired deadline: Final wages are due within 6 calendar days of discharge (Texas Labor Code § 61.014).
Vacation payout: Payout of accrued leave is required under the Texas Payday Law only if promised by the employer in a written policy or agreement; if no such policy exists, no payout is owed. A policy may instead specify forfeiture of unused leave on separation (with limited carve-outs, e.g. for layoffs or employees giving advance notice), and such a policy is enforced as written.		Source →
UtahQuit deadline: If the employee has no written contract for a definite period and resigns, wages earned and unpaid (together with any deposit held by the employer) become due and payable on the next regular payday.
Fired deadline: Unpaid wages become due immediately and must be paid within 24 hours of separation (by hand delivery, by mailing postmarked no more than one day after separation, or by initiating direct deposit within 24 hours). If not paid within 24 hours of a written demand, wages continue accruing at the same rate as a penalty, up to a maximum of 60 days.
Vacation payout: Vacation, holiday pay, sick leave, PTO, severance and bonuses count as wages "if due under an agreement with the employer or under a policy of the employer" (Utah Admin. Code R610-3-4(B)(1)); absent such a policy or agreement, Utah does not independently require a payout.		Source →
VermontQuit deadline: On the last regular payday, or if there is no regular payday, on the following Friday.
Fired deadline: Within 72 hours of discharge.
Vacation payout: Not specified by statute — this section (21 V.S.A. § 342) covers payment timing and methods only, not vacation time obligations.		Source →
VirginiaQuit deadline: On or before the date on which the employee would have been paid had employment not been terminated (i.e., the next regular payday) — the same rule applies whether the employee quit or was discharged.
Fired deadline: Same as quit_deadline — on or before the next regular payday.
Vacation payout: Not specified by statute; the law requires payment of "all wages due" for work performed but doesn't address whether accrued vacation counts.		Source →
WashingtonQuit deadline: At the end of the established pay period (the next regular payday) — the statute applies the same rule whether the employee quit or was discharged: "When any employee shall cease to work for an employer, whether by discharge or by voluntary withdrawal, the wages due him or her...shall be paid...at the end of the established pay period."
Fired deadline: Same as quit_deadline — at the end of the established pay period.
Vacation payout: Not specified by statute.		Source →
West VirginiaQuit deadline: On or before the next regular payday on which the wages would otherwise be due and payable — the same rule applies whether the employee quit or was discharged.
Fired deadline: Same as quit_deadline — on or before the next regular payday.
Vacation payout: Accrued fringe benefits (which can include vacation) that are due but, under the terms of an agreement, payable at a future date or upon ascertainable conditions, follow those contractual terms rather than the standard final-paycheck deadline.		Source →
WisconsinQuit deadline: In accordance with the employer's regular pay schedule — the same rule applies whether the employee quit or was discharged.
Fired deadline: Same as quit_deadline — in accordance with the employer's regular pay schedule. If payment isn't made, the employee can request it, and may file a wage claim with the Department of Workforce Development if not paid within 6 days of that request.
Vacation payout: If the employer has a written vacation policy with no written forfeiture clause, the employer must pay out earned, unused vacation; employers aren't required to offer vacation benefits at all.		Source →
WyomingQuit deadline: By the employer's next regular payday (W.S. 27-4-104(a)) — the same rule applies whether the employee quit or was discharged.
Fired deadline: Same as quit_deadline — by the next regular payday.
Vacation payout: Wyoming law does not require employers to offer vacation or pay out unused vacation time at separation (W.S. 27-4-507), but if the employer's policy promises a payout, the employer must honor it.		Source →
District of ColumbiaQuit deadline: On the next regular payday or within 7 days of quitting/resigning, whichever is earlier.
Fired deadline: No later than the working day following the discharge.
Vacation payout: Not specified by statute.		Source →

Employment Rights Act 1996 (notice pay) and the statutory holiday entitlement rules (Working Time Regulations 1998, as reflected in current gov.uk guidance)

Notice pay
Statutory minimum notice an employer must give: "at least one week's notice if employed between one month and 2 years; one week's notice for each year if employed between 2 and 12 years; 12 weeks' notice if employed for 12 years or more." An employer may give more than this statutory minimum (per the contract) but not less. The employer should either pay the employee through the notice period or pay in lieu of notice; notice pay is based on the average earned per week over the 12 weeks before the notice period starts.
Holiday pay on leaving
"The only time someone can get paid in place of taking statutory leave (known as 'payment in lieu') is when they leave their job. Employers must pay for untaken statutory leave, even if the worker is dismissed for gross misconduct." Statutory leave entitlement is 5.6 weeks (28 days for a worker on a 5-day week) a year.

Evidence to gather

  • Resignation or termination letter
  • Last payslip
  • Timesheets
  • Holiday balance

Steps

  1. Gather your evidence (see the list above).
  2. Send a written complaint to the business or provider, citing the rule for your region from "Your rights" above.
  3. If unresolved by their deadline, escalate using the route named in "Your rights" for your region.
  4. If still unresolved, consider your region's ombudsman, regulator, or small claims court — see "Your rights" for the specific option.
