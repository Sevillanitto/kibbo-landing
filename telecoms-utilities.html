Guides, by region

29 in-depth guides — kept separate by jurisdiction, never merged, since telecom and utility regulation differs sharply by region.

Comcast, AT&T, or Verizon Ignoring You? How to File an FCC Complaint That Actually Gets a Response

The FCC's informal complaint process skips the customer-service loop and puts your provider on a 30-day legal clock to respond in writing — no fee, no lawyer.

Facing a Utility Shutoff? Your State's Public Utilities Commission Has Real Power Here

Electricity, gas and water are regulated by your state's PUC — with real power over billing disputes and shutoffs. Cold-weather rules, the notice you're owed, and how a case can pause disconnection.

How to Spot and Dispute Cramming on Your US Telecom Bill

A line on your phone bill says "service fee," "membership," or "other charges" and you don't recognize it. That can be cramming — but not every fee is.

The Slamming Complaint Process: What to Do When Your US Phone Provider Was Changed Without Your Consent

You never authorised the switch, but another carrier is billing you. Federal slamming rules provide a specific remedy — primarily for wireline telephone service.

How to Cancel Internet or TV Without Paying an Early Termination Fee

An early termination fee isn't automatically illegal because service is slow or you're moving. The contract, disclosures, and applicable law all matter.

Internet Outages in the US: When Can You Demand a Billing Credit?

Your internet was down for days, but the provider refuses a credit. There's no single federal rule requiring a prorated refund for every outage.

US Number Portability: How to Keep Your Phone Number When Switching Carriers

You can owe your old carrier money and still have important number-portability protections — but porting doesn't erase a valid balance or contract fee.

Broadband or Mobile Complaint Going Nowhere? Here's How Ofcom's Complaint System Actually Works

UK telecoms providers must belong to a free arbitration scheme you can use once you've given them a fair chance — including the wait-time drop to 6 weeks from 8 April 2026.

Got a Shock Energy Bill? Ofgem's 12-Month Rule Might Mean You Don't Have to Pay It

A catch-up bill going back years is one of the most stressful surprises a supplier can send. Ofgem's back-billing rule (SLC 21BA) can mean you owe nothing older than 12 months.

How to Escalate an Unresolved Energy or Broadband Complaint to the Ombudsman

A free arbitration route that's genuinely binding on the company — but energy and telecoms now run on different clocks.

Struggling With Energy Bills or Switching Supplier? Start With Citizens Advice, Not Your Supplier's Retention Team

UK energy consumers have real, lesser-known protections — from the Debt Assignment Protocol to Supplier of Last Resort.

Got a Call From "Your Bank" or HMRC? Here's How to Actually Verify It

Your bank's name and number on your screen isn't proof — caller ID spoofing can fake almost anything.

Ofgem's 12-Month Back-Billing Rule: Why You May Not Owe That Catch-Up Energy Bill

A bill arrives out of nowhere for energy used over a year ago. If the delay was your supplier's fault, you may not owe it.

Mid-Contract Price Rises: Your Rights Under Ofcom's CPI/RPI Ban

"CPI + 3.9%" used to mean an unpredictable price rise every year. Since January 2025, that formula is banned in new UK telecom contracts.

Ofcom's Automatic Compensation Scheme: What You're Owed for Broadband Delays

Delayed repairs, missed engineer appointments, and delayed activations are supposed to trigger automatic payments — no claim form required.

Forced Prepayment Meter Installation: Your Protections Under Ofgem's Code of Practice

Energy debt doesn't give a supplier a free pass to force a prepayment meter into your home — a mandatory code sets out what they must do first.

How to Escalate an Unresolved Energy or Telecom Complaint to the Ombudsman

There's no single "Energy and Telecoms Ombudsman" in the UK — energy and telecom complaints go through two entirely separate schemes.

Challenging Estimated Energy Bills in the EU: Your Rights Under the Electricity Market Directive

A supplier bases your bill on an inflated estimate instead of your actual reading. EU energy rules give you more leverage here than most consumers realise.

Telecom Contract Lock-In in the EU: The 24-Month Limit Under the European Electronic Communications Code

Some operators try to stretch commitment periods past two years through device financing. EU law sets a hard ceiling on this.

Your Fibre Isn't Hitting the Advertised Speed: Your Rights Under the EU Open Internet Regulation

EU law gives you a real remedy for a persistent speed shortfall, but the measurement method matters more than most people realise.

Facing an Energy Disconnection in the EU? Your Rights Before Supply Can Be Cut Off

A supplier moving straight to disconnection without warning skips steps the law requires them to take first, in most EU countries.

Cancelling Insurance or Maintenance Add-Ons Bundled With Your Energy Bill in the EU

Home insurance or boiler cover you never asked for shows up on your energy bill. There are two separate EU rights here, and which one applies changes what you can do.

The TIO Mandate: How to Force ISPs to Cancel Contracts and Waive Termination Fees Legally

If your NBN or mobile plan fails to deliver, or the provider stonewalls your cancellation, you don't have to pay an early termination fee.

The Smartphone Guarantee: Enforcing ACL Rights Beyond the Manufacturer's 12-Month Warranty

A premium phone failing at 14 or 18 months isn't yours to pay for. Under the ACL, that figure is a marketing limit, not a legal cutoff.

Bill Shock and the TCP Code: Your Right to Data Usage Alerts in Australia

A huge bill for extra data or roaming arrives, and you never got a warning. The TCP Code requires those warnings — and telcos have been penalised for skipping them.

NBN Problems? Why Your Internet Provider, Not NBN Co, Is Legally Responsible

Your retail provider blames NBN Co's network. Under Australian Consumer Law, that's not who you have a contract with — or who owes you a remedy.

Signed an Energy Contract at Your Door? Your 10-Day Cooling-Off Right in Australia

A salesperson at your door talked you into switching on the spot. Two separate legal frameworks give you a real window to walk away.

How to Complain to the Telecommunications Industry Ombudsman (TIO)

Every Australian phone and internet provider must belong to the TIO scheme by law — and the fee structure gives them a real incentive to fix your problem fast.

Sky-High Energy or Water Bill? How to Get a Faulty Meter Tested in Australia

A bill shows consumption an average household couldn't plausibly generate. There's a clear, free path to get the meter tested.
