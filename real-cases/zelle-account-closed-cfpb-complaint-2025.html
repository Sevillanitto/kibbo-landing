CFPB Complaint United States Financial & Banking

A Zelle User Says the Bank Blamed Them Repeatedly, Then Closed Their Account Without Warning

One of 13,445 complaints filed under the same issue code on a single day, and one of the few written in the consumer's own words.

Kibbo Editorial Team · Case reference: CFPB complaint 11650441

The Case

On 18 January 2025 a consumer filed a complaint against Early Warning Services, LLC, the operator of Zelle. The consumer says money was taken from them more than once. Each time they contacted their bank or Zelle, they say they were told it was their own fault and that nobody could help. They say the bank then closed their account, with no warning and no reason given.

The complaint was filed under the issue "Other transaction problem" in the category of domestic money transfers.

The Consumer

The consumer does not say how the money left the account, or whether they approved the payments. That matters, because the rules differ sharply depending on whether a transfer was unauthorised or whether the consumer authorised it and was then deceived. The complaint does not settle this, and we do not guess.

The Institution

Early Warning Services, LLC received the complaint on the same day it was filed. It responded within the CFPB's timeframe and closed the complaint "with explanation". The public data does not show what the explanation was.

What the Law Said

For electronic transfers the consumer did not authorise, the Electronic Fund Transfer Act and its Regulation E set error-resolution duties for the bank, including investigating within set deadlines. Transfers the consumer authorised, including payments made after being deceived by a scammer, are treated differently, and protection is much weaker.

Whether a bank may close an account without notice depends mainly on the account agreement and state law. The complaint does not include that agreement.

What Was Decided

There was no ruling. The company reported that it closed the complaint with an explanation, which in the CFPB's categories means no monetary or non-monetary relief was recorded. This response is self-reported by the company and not verified by the CFPB.

Why It Matters

This complaint was filed on 18 January 2025, inside the week when complaints under this same issue code jumped from a few hundred a month to more than 42,000 in January alone. On 17 January there were 13,445 in a single day. Kibbo's sample of narratives from that week found that 61.6% of Block, Inc. narratives and 55.9% of Early Warning Services narratives were exact copies of another narrative. The surge was first reported by Consumer Reports, which linked it to social media.

This complaint is different. Its text matches no other narrative in the archive. It is a reminder that inside a wave of copied text, individual people with individual problems are still filing complaints, and that the copied volume makes them harder to see.

What You Can Learn From This

  • Say how the transfer happened. "Unauthorised" and "I sent it but was tricked" lead to different rights; state which one applies.
  • Ask your bank in writing for the reason for any account closure, and ask for the date and the clause of the account agreement it relies on.
  • Keep dates, amounts and the names of anyone you spoke to. A complaint with specifics is easier for a company to investigate than a general one.
