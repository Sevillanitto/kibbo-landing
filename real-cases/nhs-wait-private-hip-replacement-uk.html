"Unless You Go Private, There's an 18-Month Waiting List" — a £28,000 Decision

Rosemary Duff, 71, waited a month to see her GP and four more to see a consultant — only to be told her real options were an 18-month wait or paying privately. She paid nearly £28,000 to have both hips replaced.

Kibbo Editorial Team·Norwich, United Kingdom

The Case

Rosemary Duff, a retired market researcher from Norwich, began experiencing hip pain that worsened after the first Covid lockdown. NHS physiotherapy slowed the deterioration for a time, but a year later the pain had increased to the point where she struggled to walk far or climb stairs.

The Patient

Duff waited a month simply to see her GP, then a further four months for a consultant appointment. When she finally saw the consultant, she was told: "Unless you go private, there's an 18-month waiting list." She had not been expecting to need or fund private treatment.

The System's Position

The NHS trust did not dispute the 18-month estimate — this reflects the well-documented, verified pressure on NHS orthopaedic waiting lists nationally, not a failure specific to Duff's case. The consultant's framing simply made the trade-off explicit and immediate.

What She Decided

Duff chose to dip into her savings rather than wait. She spent almost £28,000 having both hips replaced at a private hospital run by Circle Health Group.

Why It Matters

This case puts a concrete price on something waiting-list statistics can only show in the abstract: for many UK patients, "free at the point of use" and "the only realistic option" are not the same thing. When the wait is long enough, the NHS's zero-pound sticker price coexists with a very real five-figure private bill for anyone who can't or won't wait.

What You Can Learn From This

  • Ask your consultant directly for the current specific waiting time for your specific procedure at your specific trust — national averages can be many months off from your local reality in either direction.
  • NHS patients can request treatment at a different, faster hospital under the NHS's own "patient choice" right — this doesn't require going private, and is worth asking about explicitly before paying out of pocket.
  • If you do go private, ask whether the NHS will honor a shorter subsequent waiting time for any follow-up care related to the procedure — this varies by trust and isn't always volunteered.

Documents & Evidence

Reported in UK national press coverage of patients turning to private healthcare due to NHS waiting-list pressure.
